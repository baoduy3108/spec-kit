# Microeconomics & Market Behavior

Microeconomics studies how **individuals, households, and firms make decisions** and interact in markets — how prices form, how resources allocate, and why. Its central engine is **supply and demand**, and its core mindset is **marginal thinking**. These ideas explain everything from grocery prices to labor wages.

## Supply and Demand (The Core Model)

- **Demand** — how much buyers want at each price; the demand curve slopes **down** (lower price → more demanded). Driven by preferences, income, prices of related goods, expectations.
- **Supply** — how much sellers offer at each price; the supply curve slopes **up** (higher price → more supplied). Driven by costs, technology, number of sellers.
- **Equilibrium** — where supply meets demand; the market-clearing price and quantity. Above it → surplus (price falls); below it → shortage (price rises). Markets tend toward equilibrium.
- **Shifts vs movements** — a price change *moves along* a curve; a change in other factors (income, cost) *shifts* the whole curve, changing equilibrium. Distinguishing these is essential.

## Price Elasticity

**Elasticity** measures responsiveness to price changes:
- **Elastic demand** — quantity responds a lot to price (luxuries, many substitutes). Raising price *lowers* total revenue.
- **Inelastic demand** — quantity barely responds (necessities, few substitutes — insulin, gas). Raising price *raises* revenue.
- Elasticity drives pricing, tax incidence (who really bears a tax — the less elastic side), and revenue strategy. A key practical concept.

## Marginal Thinking

The economist's signature tool: decisions are made **at the margin** — comparing the *additional* benefit vs *additional* cost of one more unit:
- **Marginal utility** (benefit of one more) usually **diminishes**; **marginal cost** (cost of one more) usually rises.
- Rational agents act while **marginal benefit ≥ marginal cost**, stopping where they meet. Firms maximize profit where **marginal revenue = marginal cost**.
- **Sunk costs** are irrelevant to marginal decisions (only future costs/benefits matter). Thinking at the margin, not in totals, is the core discipline.

## Surplus & Efficiency

- **Consumer surplus** — the gap between what buyers *would* pay and what they *do*. **Producer surplus** — the gap between the price and sellers' costs.
- Total surplus measures market **efficiency**; free-market equilibrium (under ideal conditions) maximizes it. Price controls, taxes, and monopoly create **deadweight loss** (lost surplus).

## Market Structures

Competition ranges across a spectrum:
- **Perfect competition** — many small firms, identical products, price-*takers*; efficient, zero long-run economic profit.
- **Monopolistic competition** — many firms, differentiated products (restaurants, brands).
- **Oligopoly** — few large firms, interdependent (strategic — see game theory).
- **Monopoly** — one seller, price-*maker*; restricts output, raises price, creates deadweight loss (hence antitrust regulation).

Structure determines pricing power, efficiency, and outcomes.

## Externalities & Market Failure

Markets can **fail** to reach efficient outcomes:
- **Externalities** — costs/benefits imposed on third parties (pollution = negative; vaccination/education = positive). Unpriced, they cause over- or under-production. Fixes: taxes (Pigouvian), subsidies, regulation, tradable permits.
- **Public goods, information asymmetry, and monopoly power** are other failures justifying intervention.

Reason about markets using **supply and demand** (equilibrium, shifts), **elasticity** (responsiveness, revenue, tax incidence), **marginal thinking** (benefit vs cost of one more, ignore sunk costs), **surplus/efficiency**, **market structure** (competition → monopoly and its pricing power), and **externalities/market failure**. Microeconomics is the logic of how self-interested decisions, mediated by prices, allocate scarce resources — and when and why that logic breaks down.
