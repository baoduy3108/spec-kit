# Chemical Reactions & Stoichiometry

Stoichiometry is the quantitative bookkeeping of chemistry — using balanced equations to calculate *how much* reactant is needed and *how much* product forms. It rests on one bedrock principle: **conservation of mass** (atoms are neither created nor destroyed in a reaction, just rearranged).

## Balancing Equations

A chemical equation must have the **same number of each atom on both sides** (mass conservation). Balancing adjusts **coefficients** (never subscripts — that would change the substances) until every element matches:
- `H₂ + O₂ → H₂O` is unbalanced; balanced: `2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O`.
- The coefficients express the **ratio** in which substances react — the heart of stoichiometry.

Balance systematically (often start with the most complex molecule, save O and H for last).

## The Mole & Molar Mass

Chemists count particles by the **mole** — a fixed huge number (Avogadro's number, ~6.022×10²³) of particles. It bridges the atomic scale to grams you can weigh:
- **Molar mass** (g/mol) = the mass of one mole of a substance (sum of atomic masses). Water is ~18 g/mol.
- **moles = mass ÷ molar mass** — the key conversion between grams (measurable) and moles (countable, which reactions actually care about).

The mole is the unit that makes reaction math work, because reactions happen in whole-number *particle* ratios, not gram ratios.

## Stoichiometric Calculations

The balanced equation's coefficients are **mole ratios**. To find how much product a given reactant yields:
1. **Convert** the known mass → moles (÷ molar mass).
2. **Apply the mole ratio** from the balanced equation → moles of the target.
3. **Convert** back to grams (× molar mass), or to volume/particles as needed.

This "grams → moles → ratio → moles → grams" chain is the core stoichiometry workflow.

## Limiting Reagent & Yield

When reactants aren't in exact stoichiometric proportion:
- The **limiting reagent** runs out first and *caps* how much product can form; the others are in **excess**. Identify it by comparing available mole ratios to the required ratio.
- **Theoretical yield** — the maximum product from the limiting reagent (by stoichiometry).
- **Actual yield** — what you really get (less, due to side reactions, losses, incomplete reaction).
- **Percent yield** = actual ÷ theoretical × 100% — a measure of reaction efficiency.

Limiting-reagent thinking is essential in the lab and industry (don't waste expensive reactants; size batches correctly).

## Reaction Types

Recognizing patterns helps predict products:
- **Synthesis (combination)** — A + B → AB.
- **Decomposition** — AB → A + B.
- **Combustion** — fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O (+ energy).
- **Single/double displacement** — atoms/ions swap partners (e.g. precipitation reactions).
- **Acid-base (neutralization)** — acid + base → salt + water; proton transfer.
- **Redox (oxidation-reduction)** — electron transfer; one species is oxidized (loses electrons), another reduced (gains). Underlies combustion, corrosion, batteries, metabolism. Track **oxidation states** to identify and balance them.

## Beyond Amounts

Stoichiometry answers *how much*; other chemistry answers *whether/how fast/how far*:
- **Thermochemistry** — energy absorbed/released (see thermodynamics-and-entropy).
- **Kinetics** — reaction rates.
- **Equilibrium** — reversible reactions balancing forward/backward.

Stoichiometry is chemistry's **accounting**: balance the equation for conservation of mass, convert masses to moles, apply the coefficient ratios, respect the limiting reagent, and compute yields — turning "these chemicals react" into precise, predictable quantities.
