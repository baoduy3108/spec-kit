# Survival & Crafting Design

Survival games put the player in a hostile world with nothing and task them with **gathering, crafting, and enduring** their way to security and power. The core loop — *need → gather → craft → survive → expand* — is deeply compelling when tuned right, turning scarcity and vulnerability into a satisfying climb from desperate to dominant.

## The Core Resource Loop

Everything orbits **resources and scarcity**:
- **Gather** raw materials (wood, stone, food, ore) from the world.
- **Craft** them into tools, which enable gathering *better* resources, which craft *better* tools — a self-reinforcing progression engine.
- **Scarcity creates tension** — early resources are precious, every choice matters. As you progress, scarcity eases and the fantasy shifts from *surviving* to *thriving/building*.

Tuning gather rates, crafting costs, and resource distribution is the balance heart: too scarce = tedious grind; too abundant = no tension.

## Needs & Meters

The "survival" pressure comes from **depleting needs** the player must maintain:
- **Hunger, thirst, temperature, stamina, health, sleep** — meters that drain over time and demand attention.
- They create a **constant background pressure** and steady goals (find food, build shelter, make a fire).
- **Danger vs tedium** — needs should create meaningful decisions and tension, not annoying micromanagement. Many games ramp needs slowly, or offer difficulty toggles, so they're a spice, not a chore. Over-punishing needs frustrate; too-gentle ones feel pointless.

## Crafting & Tech Trees

- **Crafting recipes** gate content: you can't make X until you have Y and the knowledge/station for it.
- **Tech/progression tree** — unlock recipes by leveling, finding blueprints, or building workstations, pacing the power climb.
- **Discovery vs recipe list** — some games hide recipes (experiment to discover), others show them as goals. Each shapes the exploration/planning feel.
- Crafting should feel like **meaningful advancement**, each tier visibly better (stone → iron → advanced).

## Base Building

Most survival games let players **build a home base** — shelter from threats/weather, storage, crafting stations, defenses. Base building provides:
- **A safety anchor** and a canvas for creativity/ownership (players love "their" base).
- **Investment** — a reason to defend territory and a visible record of progress.
- Design considerations: buildable grid/snapping, structural rules, defense vs threats, and giving bases *purpose* (not just decoration).

## Cycles & Threats

- **Day-night cycle** — days for gathering/building, nights for danger (monsters, cold). This rhythm structures play and creates recurring tension and preparation loops.
- **Escalating threats** — weather, enemies, events that scale so the world stays dangerous as you grow stronger.
- **Risk-reward exploration** — venturing farther/deeper yields better resources but more danger; the pull to explore vs the safety of home is a core tension.

## The Power Curve

Survival's arc is a deliberate journey from **vulnerable to powerful**:
- **Early game** — desperate, scarce, every night scary; core survival tension.
- **Mid game** — established base, steady resources, expanding capabilities.
- **Late game** — secure and powerful; the fantasy shifts to mega-building, automation, or conquering the world's biggest threats.

Design the curve so the early tension is real but not hopeless, and later stages offer new goals (the "what now?" problem — many survival games struggle to keep late-game compelling; answer it with automation, exploration frontiers, or endgame threats).

## Multiplayer Note

Many survival games are co-op/PvP; that adds cooperation (division of labor), shared bases, and PvP raiding tension — powerful but requiring persistence, anti-grief, and balance considerations.

Design survival-crafting around a **tight gather→craft→survive→expand loop under meaningful scarcity**, with needs that pressure without nagging, a satisfying crafting/tech climb, base building for ownership, and day-night/threat cycles pacing a vulnerable-to-powerful journey. The magic is turning "I have nothing" into "I built all this."
