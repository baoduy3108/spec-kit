# Trivia & Quiz Game Design

Trivia games (Jeopardy!, Trivial Pursuit, QuizUp, HQ Trivia, Jackbox's Trivia Murder Party) test **knowledge** through questions. They seem simple but require real design craft: writing fair, engaging questions, balancing difficulty and breadth, and wrapping it all in mechanics that make *answering questions* exciting and social. The content — the questions themselves — is the game.

## Question Writing (The Core Craft)

The heart of any trivia game is **well-written questions**:
- **Clarity and unambiguity** — a question must have one clearly correct answer, worded so it's not accidentally tricky or open to interpretation. Ambiguous questions cause disputes and feel unfair.
- **Interesting** — good trivia questions are *fun to know the answer to* ("oh, cool!"), not just obscure. The "aha" or "I knew that!" is the reward.
- **Verifiable and accurate** — facts must be correct and current (mis-fact-checked questions destroy trust).
- **Fair difficulty calibration** — knowing how hard a question actually is (not too easy, not impossibly obscure). Playtesting reveals true difficulty.

Question quality *is* game quality.

## Category Breadth & Fairness

- **Broad categories** — variety (history, science, pop culture, sports, geography…) so different players' knowledge shines and no single expertise dominates. Breadth is fairness and inclusion.
- **Avoiding bias** — questions skewed to one region, era, or niche exclude players. Balance for the intended audience.
- **Difficulty distribution** — a mix of easy (everyone gets some), medium, and hard, so play feels achievable yet challenging.

## Question Formats

Variety keeps it fresh:
- **Multiple choice** — accessible, easy to score, gives a chance to reason/guess (and enables clever wrong-answer "distractors").
- **Open answer / free response** — harder, more satisfying, but needs answer-matching tolerance (typos, synonyms — see search forgiveness).
- **True/false, buzz-in (speed), ordering, matching, image/audio questions** — different formats test different things and vary the rhythm.

## Scoring & Risk Mechanics

Beyond right/wrong, **mechanics add strategy and drama**:
- **Points by difficulty** — harder questions worth more.
- **Speed bonuses** — faster correct answers score more (buzz-in tension).
- **Wagering/risk** (Jeopardy's Daily Double, betting) — risk-reward decisions that add strategy and comebacks.
- **Streaks/multipliers, lifelines, elimination** — mechanics that create tension, comebacks, and stakes so it's more than a flat Q&A.

These turn a quiz into a *game* with drama and decisions.

## Pacing & Time Pressure

- **Time limits** per question create tension and prevent lookup/stalling; the ticking clock is a core thrill.
- **Rhythm** — quick questions keep energy high; a mix of pace and dramatic reveals (building suspense before the answer). Don't let it drag.
- **Reveal moments** — the tension of the answer reveal is a key beat, especially in social/show formats.

## Content Freshness & Question Banks

- **Large question banks** — enough questions that players don't quickly see repeats (repetition kills replayability). Content volume is a real production cost.
- **Freshness** — updating with new/topical questions, especially for live/recurring formats (HQ Trivia's daily shows). Categories like current events go stale fast.
- **Difficulty tagging and rotation** to serve varied players and avoid staleness.

## Social & Party Play

Trivia shines **socially** (see party-game-design):
- **Multiplayer/competitive** — the fun of testing knowledge against friends, buzz-in races, and trash talk.
- **Accessible** — anyone can play; knowledge is widely (if unevenly) distributed, so it's inclusive.
- Live/host formats (Jackbox, HQ) add showmanship and shared-moment energy.

Design trivia games around **excellent, fair, interesting question-writing**, **broad balanced categories**, **varied formats**, **scoring and risk mechanics** that add drama and strategy, tight **time-pressure pacing** with satisfying reveals, a **fresh, large question bank**, and **social play** — because in trivia, the questions are the content and the game: write them well, calibrate them fairly, and wrap them in mechanics that make knowing (and guessing) thrilling.
