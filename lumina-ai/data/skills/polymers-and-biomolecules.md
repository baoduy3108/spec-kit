# Polymers & Biomolecules (Advanced Chemistry)

Polymers are giant molecules built from repeating units — the basis of plastics *and* of life itself. This advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) topic connects organic chemistry (see organic-chemistry-basics) to materials and biology, covering synthetic polymers and the four great classes of biological molecules.

## Polymerization

Polymers form by joining many small **monomers** into long chains:
- **Addition polymerization** — monomers with C=C double bonds add together with no byproduct (ethene → polyethene; also PVC, polystyrene, polypropylene). The double bond opens and links.
- **Condensation polymerization** — monomers join with the loss of a small molecule (usually water); needs two functional groups per monomer (esters → polyesters, amides → polyamides/nylon). Also how biological polymers form.

## Synthetic Polymers (Plastics)

- **Plastics** — polyethene, PVC, polystyrene, PET, nylon, etc. Properties (flexible/rigid, thermoplastic/thermoset) depend on structure and chain interactions.
- **Thermoplastics** soften on heating (recyclable); **thermosets** are permanently cross-linked (rigid).
- **Environmental issues** — non-biodegradability, recycling, and biodegradable/green polymers are increasingly important themes.

## Biomolecules: The Four Classes

Life's chemistry is built on four classes of (mostly polymeric) molecules:

**1. Carbohydrates** (đường, tinh bột)
- Sugars and their polymers; monomers are **monosaccharides** (glucose, fructose).
- **Disaccharides** (sucrose) and **polysaccharides** (starch — energy storage in plants; glycogen — in animals; cellulose — structural). Made/broken by condensation/hydrolysis. Primary energy source.

**2. Lipids** (chất béo)
- Fats, oils, phospholipids, steroids — **nonpolar**, insoluble in water.
- **Triglycerides** (glycerol + 3 fatty acids, joined by ester bonds) store energy; **phospholipids** form cell membranes; provide insulation and signaling.

**3. Proteins** (protein)
- Polymers of **amino acids** joined by **peptide (amide) bonds** (condensation). 20 amino acids in countless sequences.
- **Structure levels** — primary (sequence) → secondary (helices/sheets) → tertiary (3D fold) → quaternary (multiple chains). Shape determines function.
- **Functions** — enzymes (catalysts), structure, transport (haemoglobin), antibodies, hormones. The workhorses of life.

**4. Nucleic Acids** (axit nucleic — DNA/RNA)
- Polymers of **nucleotides** (a sugar + phosphate + nitrogenous base).
- **DNA** — double helix storing genetic information (base pairs A-T, G-C — see cell-biology-and-heredity); **RNA** — carries and translates it into proteins (see molecular-genetics).

## Common Threads

- **Condensation builds, hydrolysis breaks** — biological polymers form by removing water (condensation) and break by adding it (hydrolysis, e.g. digestion).
- **Structure determines function** — from a plastic's flexibility to a protein's catalytic shape, the arrangement of monomers dictates properties.

Master polymers and biomolecules via **polymerization** (addition of C=C monomers vs condensation with water loss), **synthetic plastics** (thermoplastics vs thermosets, environmental impact), and the **four biomolecule classes** — **carbohydrates** (energy, glucose polymers), **lipids** (fats, membranes, ester-linked), **proteins** (amino acids via peptide bonds; structure = function), and **nucleic acids** (nucleotides storing genetic info). The unifying idea — condensation builds monomers into functional polymers whose structure dictates their role — bridges gifted-exam chemistry and biology.
