# Oscillations & Waves (THPT Physics)

Oscillations (repetitive back-and-forth motion) and waves (oscillations that travel through space) are a major THPT physics topic. They describe pendulums, springs, sound, light, and much of nature's rhythm. The core model is **simple harmonic motion**, and waves are oscillations propagating energy without transporting matter.

## Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

The fundamental oscillation model:
- **SHM** — motion where the restoring force is proportional to displacement and directed back toward equilibrium (F = −kx). Produces smooth sinusoidal motion.
- **Key quantities**: **amplitude (A)** — maximum displacement; **period (T)** — time for one cycle; **frequency (f = 1/T)** — cycles per second (Hz); **angular frequency ω = 2πf**.
- Position varies as **x = A·cos(ωt + φ)** (sinusoidal — see trigonometry). Velocity and acceleration are also sinusoidal, out of phase.
- **Energy** oscillates between kinetic and potential, total conserved (see newtonian-mechanics).

## Pendulums & Springs

Two classic SHM systems (common THPT problems):
- **Mass-spring** — T = 2π√(m/k). Period depends on mass and spring stiffness, *not* amplitude.
- **Simple pendulum** — T = 2π√(L/g) (small angles). Period depends on length and gravity, *not* mass or amplitude — the isochronism Galileo noted.
- Both convert between kinetic and potential energy each cycle.

## Wave Properties

A **wave** is a disturbance that propagates, carrying **energy** (not matter):
- **Wavelength (λ)** — distance between successive crests. **Frequency (f)** — oscillations per second. **Amplitude** — height (relates to energy/intensity).
- **Wave speed**: **v = f·λ** — the fundamental wave equation. Speed depends on the medium.
- **Period** T = 1/f, as in oscillations.

## Types of Waves

- **Transverse** — oscillation perpendicular to travel direction (light, waves on a string, EM waves — see electromagnetism).
- **Longitudinal** — oscillation parallel to travel (sound: compressions and rarefactions in air).
- **Mechanical waves** need a medium (sound, water); **electromagnetic waves** don't (light travels through vacuum).

## Superposition & Interference

When waves overlap, their displacements **add** (superposition):
- **Constructive interference** — in-phase waves reinforce (bigger amplitude).
- **Destructive interference** — out-of-phase waves cancel (smaller or zero).
- This produces interference patterns (a key evidence that light is a wave — Young's double slit) and beats (sound).

## Standing Waves & Resonance

- **Standing waves** — interference of a wave with its reflection creates fixed **nodes** (no motion) and **antinodes** (max motion); the wave appears to stand still. Occurs on strings and in pipes at specific frequencies (**harmonics**).
- **Resonance** — driving a system at its **natural frequency** produces large-amplitude oscillation (why a swing pumps higher at the right timing, why bridges can fail, how instruments and radios work). A crucial, widely-tested concept.

## Sound

- **Sound** — a longitudinal pressure wave through a medium. **Frequency** → pitch; **amplitude** → loudness (decibels, logarithmic — see exponentials-and-logarithms).
- **Speed** varies with medium (~343 m/s in air, faster in water/solids). **Doppler effect** — frequency shifts with relative motion (approaching siren higher-pitched). Resonance in pipes/strings gives musical tones.

Master THPT oscillations and waves via **SHM** (amplitude, period, frequency, ω; sinusoidal motion and energy exchange), **pendulums and springs** (T formulas), **wave properties** (v = f·λ), **transverse vs longitudinal** types, **superposition and interference** (constructive/destructive), **standing waves and resonance** (nodes, natural frequency), and **sound**. The unifying idea: oscillations repeat in time, and waves carry that oscillation — and its energy — through space.
