# Opportunity Solution Tree (OST)

The Opportunity Solution Tree (from Teresa Torres) is a **visual map** that keeps product discovery **outcome-driven** — connecting a business/product **outcome** you want, down to the customer **opportunities** (needs, pains, desires) that could move it, then to candidate **solutions**, and finally to **experiments** that test them. It's a structured antidote to the most common product failure: **jumping straight to building features** without knowing what problem they solve (see product-discovery, jobs-to-be-done, north-star-metric).

## The Problem: Feature-First Thinking

Teams constantly leap from "we should build X" to building it — skipping *why*. This produces a **feature factory** (shipping lots, moving no metric), because the features aren't tied to a real customer need or a target outcome. The OST forces a **chain of reasoning** from outcome → need → solution → evidence, so every solution traces back to a real opportunity and a desired result.

## The Four Levels of the Tree

Top to bottom:
1. **Outcome (the root)** — the single **measurable business/product outcome** you're trying to influence (e.g. "increase weekly active retention"), ideally tied to a North Star (see north-star-metric). Everything hangs off this.
2. **Opportunities (the branches)** — customer **needs, pain points, and desires** (unmet jobs — see jobs-to-be-done) discovered through **research/interviews**, that — if addressed — could drive the outcome. Structure them (bigger opportunities branch into sub-opportunities). This is the heart: a **map of the problem space**, grounded in real customer insight.
3. **Solutions (the leaves)** — candidate **ideas** to address a specific opportunity. Crucially, you generate solutions **for a chosen opportunity**, not in a vacuum — and multiple solutions per opportunity, so you're not married to the first idea.
4. **Experiments** — the **tests/assumptions** to validate whether a solution actually works (see the assumption-testing / pre-mortem ideas), before fully building.

## Why It Works

- **Outcome-driven** — everything ladders up to a measurable outcome; you can't justify a solution that doesn't tie to an opportunity that drives the outcome.
- **Problem before solution** — you explore the **opportunity space** (real needs) before generating solutions, avoiding premature feature commitment.
- **Makes trade-offs visible** — you **compare opportunities** (which to pursue) and **compare solutions** within an opportunity, deliberately — instead of building whatever was loudest.
- **Continuous discovery** — the tree evolves as you learn; it's a living map, not a one-time doc.
- **Shared understanding** — the team sees *why* they're building something, from outcome to evidence.

## Design Guidance

- **Start from one measurable outcome** — not a feature or output.
- **Fill opportunities from real research** (customer interviews), not guesses — the tree is only as good as the insight.
- **Structure opportunities** (parent/child) to map the problem space.
- **Generate several solutions per chosen opportunity** — don't commit to the first idea.
- **Assess opportunities and solutions deliberately** (impact, evidence) before betting.
- **Test with experiments** — validate risky assumptions before full build.
- **Keep it living** — update the tree as discovery continues.

## Pitfalls (in understanding/using)

- **Jumping to solutions** without an opportunity/outcome → feature factory (the whole thing OST prevents).
- Filling opportunities from **assumptions** instead of real customer research → a pretty but wrong tree.
- **One solution per opportunity** → anchored on the first idea; generate alternatives.
- Solutions that **don't ladder up** to an opportunity/outcome → unjustified work.
- Treating the tree as a **one-time artifact** → it should evolve with continuous discovery.
- Confusing **outputs** (features shipped) with the **outcome** (metric moved) at the root.
