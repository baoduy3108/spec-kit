# Momentum, Collisions & Center of Mass (Advanced Physics)

Momentum conservation is one of physics' most powerful tools — it solves collision and explosion problems that force analysis can't easily touch. At the advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) and olympiad level, this extends to 2D collisions, the center of mass, and clever reference-frame techniques.

## Momentum & Impulse

- **Momentum p = mv** — a vector, "quantity of motion" (see newtonian-mechanics).
- **Impulse** = force × time = **change in momentum** (J = FΔt = Δp). A longer collision time reduces peak force (airbags, follow-through).
- **Newton's 2nd law** in its truest form: F = dp/dt.

## Conservation of Momentum (The Key Principle)

- **In the absence of external forces, total momentum is conserved** — before = after. Internal forces (during a collision/explosion) cancel in pairs (Newton's 3rd law).
- This is the master tool for **collisions and explosions**: even when energy is lost or forces are unknown/complicated, momentum still balances. Apply it per direction (x and y independently for 2D).

## Elastic vs Inelastic Collisions

- **Elastic** — kinetic energy *is* conserved (in addition to momentum). Ideal, like billiard balls or atoms. Both conservation laws together solve for final velocities.
  - **1D elastic special results** — equal masses exchange velocities; a light object bounces off a heavy one nearly reversing; the relative velocity reverses (v₁−v₂ before = −(v₁−v₂) after).
- **Inelastic** — kinetic energy is **lost** (to heat, deformation, sound); momentum still conserved.
- **Perfectly inelastic** — objects **stick together** afterward (move with common velocity); maximum KE loss. The ballistic pendulum is the classic example.

## 2D Collisions

- **Resolve momentum into components** — conserve px and py *separately*. Combined with energy conservation (if elastic), solve for the resulting directions and speeds.
- Glancing collisions, explosions into fragments — all handled by vector momentum conservation. A staple of advanced problems.

## Center of Mass

- The **center of mass (CM)** — the mass-weighted average position: **R_cm = Σmᵢrᵢ / Σmᵢ**. The system behaves, for external forces, as if all mass were concentrated at the CM.
- **CM motion** — the CM moves as though the net external force acts on the total mass (F_ext = M·a_cm). During an explosion or internal interaction, the CM keeps moving as before (internal forces don't move it).
- **Momentum = M·v_cm** — total momentum is the total mass times the CM velocity; conserved momentum means constant CM velocity.

## Reference-Frame Techniques

- **The CM frame** — analyzing collisions in the center-of-mass frame (where total momentum is zero) often dramatically simplifies them: in an elastic collision, objects just reverse their velocities in this frame.
- **Reduced mass** and frame-switching are advanced tricks (olympiad-level) that turn messy two-body problems into simpler one-body ones.

Master momentum and collisions via **impulse** (FΔt = Δp), **conservation of momentum** (the master tool for collisions/explosions, applied per direction), **elastic vs inelastic collisions** (KE conserved or lost; sticking together; 1D special cases and relative-velocity reversal), **2D collisions** (component-wise conservation), the **center of mass** (mass-weighted average, F_ext = Ma_cm, momentum = Mv_cm), and **CM-frame techniques** (simplifying collisions by choosing the right frame). Momentum conservation — valid even when forces and energy are messy — is the powerful principle at the core of gifted-exam/olympiad collision problems.
