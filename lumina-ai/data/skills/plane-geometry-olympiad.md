# Olympiad Plane Geometry

Plane (Euclidean) geometry is a classic thi-chuyên/olympiad subject demanding rigorous proof and clever construction. Problems ask you to prove points are concurrent, collinear, or that lines are tangent — solved by a toolkit of theorems and configurations, plus geometric insight.

## Triangle Centers & Configurations

- **Centroid** (medians, 2:1), **incenter** (angle bisectors, incircle), **circumcenter** (perpendicular bisectors, circumcircle), **orthocenter** (altitudes). The **Euler line** (centroid, circumcenter, orthocenter collinear) and **nine-point circle** are key facts.
- Recognizing standard configurations (incircle touch points, medial triangle, orthic triangle) unlocks many problems.

## Cyclic Quadrilaterals & Angles

The single most useful tool:
- **Inscribed-angle theorem** — angles subtending the same arc are equal; an angle in a semicircle is 90°.
- **Cyclic quadrilateral** — four points concyclic iff opposite angles sum to 180° (or equal angles subtend a chord). **Proving points concyclic** is a central technique.
- **Tangent-chord angle**, and angle chasing generally, resolves a huge fraction of problems.

## Power of a Point & Circles

- **Power of a point** — for a point P and circle, PA·PB is constant along any line through P (and equals the tangent length squared). Governs intersecting chords, secants, tangents.
- **Radical axis** and radical center for multiple circles.

## Similar Triangles & Ratios

- **Similarity** (AA, SAS, SSS) — the workhorse for length/ratio relationships and proportions.
- **Trig** — law of sines/cosines, and the extended law of sines (a/sin A = 2R).

## Key Theorems

- **Ceva's theorem** — concurrency of cevians: (BD/DC)(CE/EA)(AF/FB) = 1.
- **Menelaus's theorem** — collinearity of points on a triangle's sides (product = −1/1).
- **Ptolemy's theorem** — for a cyclic quadrilateral, AC·BD = AB·CD + AD·BC.
- **Stewart's, angle-bisector, and the intersecting-chords** theorems.

## Proof Techniques

- **Angle chasing** — track angles through the figure (often enough by itself).
- **Auxiliary constructions** — add a well-chosen line/point/circle to reveal structure.
- **Ceva/Menelaus** for concurrency/collinearity; **power of a point** for circle problems.
- **Coordinate/trig/vector bashing** — assign coordinates or use trig as a fallback (see coordinate-and-vector-geometry).
- **Transformations** — reflections, rotations, spiral similarity, homothety, inversion (advanced) to simplify.

Master olympiad plane geometry via **triangle centers and standard configurations**, **cyclic quadrilaterals and angle chasing** (the most-used tool), **power of a point**, **similar triangles**, the **key theorems** (Ceva, Menelaus, Ptolemy), and **proof techniques** (angle chasing, auxiliary constructions, transformations, and coordinate/trig bashing as fallback). Recognizing configurations and choosing the right tool for concurrency, collinearity, or concyclicity is what cracks the hardest thi-chuyên geometry.
