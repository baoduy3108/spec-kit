# Prose Style & Voice

Style is *how* you write, as distinct from *what* you write — the texture of the prose itself. Two writers can tell the same story; style is what makes one a pleasure to read. It's not decoration but craft: rhythm, precision, and voice working to make writing clear, vivid, and alive. Good style is often *invisible* — it serves the reader without calling attention to itself (except when it should).

## Sentence Rhythm & Variation

Prose has **music** — and the biggest lever is **sentence-length variation**:
- **Mix short and long.** A run of same-length sentences drones. Short sentences hit hard and quicken pace (tension, impact). Long sentences flow, build, and breathe. Varying them creates rhythm and controls pace.
- **The short sentence after long ones lands like a punch.** Use it deliberately for emphasis.
- **Read aloud** — the ear catches clunky rhythm, awkward repetition, and run-ons the eye misses. If it's hard to say, it's hard to read.

Rhythm is the most underrated element of style, and controllable with practice.

## Word Choice & Precision

- **The precise word** — the right word, not its second cousin. "Sprint" not "run fast"; "crimson" not "very red." Precise words do more work in less space. (Mark Twain: the difference between the right word and the almost-right word is lightning vs a lightning bug.)
- **Concrete over abstract** — specific, sensory, tangible language creates vivid images; abstractions ("things," "stuff," "beautiful") are vague and forgettable. Show a "chipped enamel mug," not "an object."
- **Strong verbs over adverb-propped weak ones** — "she stormed out" beats "she left angrily." Verbs carry energy; leaning on adverbs and adjectives signals a weak verb underneath.

## Cut the Filler (Concision)

**Omit needless words** (Strunk & White's core commandment). Most first-draft prose is 20-30% too long:
- Cut redundancies ("very unique," "past history," "in order to" → "to").
- Cut hedges and filler ("basically," "really," "just," "sort of," "there is/are…").
- Cut throat-clearing ("It should be noted that…"). Get to the point.
- **Tighten** — if a sentence works with fewer words, use fewer. Concision is respect for the reader and almost always improves prose.

## Tone & Register

- **Tone** — the emotional attitude (serious, wry, tender, ominous). Consistency of tone (or deliberate shifts) shapes how the reader feels.
- **Register** — formality level (formal, conversational, colloquial). Match register to the work, audience, and voice. A literary novel, a thriller, and a children's book demand different registers.
- Consistency matters — jarring register shifts (unless intentional) break immersion.

## Voice

**Voice** is the distinctive personality of the writing — the sense that *a specific someone* is telling this. It emerges from consistent choices in rhythm, diction, attitude, and worldview. Voice is what makes prose *yours* (or a character's — see character-development, point-of-view-and-narration). It's developed, not forced: through reading widely, writing lots, and noticing what feels natural and true. Distinctive voice is often what readers fall in love with.

## Editing: Clarity First, Then Beauty

Style is made in **revision**:
- **Clarity above all** — if the reader has to reread to understand, the prose failed, however pretty. Serve meaning first.
- **Then rhythm and beauty** — polish sentence music, cut filler, sharpen words, vary structure.
- **Kill your darlings** — cut even clever/beautiful bits that don't serve the whole. Self-indulgent style that shows off *at the reader's expense* is bad style.
- **Show, don't tell** where it counts (see show-dont-tell-and-scene-craft).

## The Balance

Great style is **clear, concise, rhythmic, precise, and voiced** — vivid without being purple, beautiful without being obscure. The goal isn't ornate writing; it's writing that disappears into the reader's mind as pure experience (or, when the moment calls for it, dazzles on purpose). Write to be *read*, not admired.

Develop prose style by **varying sentence rhythm**, choosing **precise, concrete words** and **strong verbs**, **cutting filler ruthlessly**, controlling **tone and register**, cultivating a genuine **voice**, and **editing for clarity first, then beauty** — reading aloud throughout. Style is craft you build sentence by sentence: the invisible art that turns information into experience.
