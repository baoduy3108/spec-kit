# Acid-Base & pH Equilibria (Advanced Chemistry)

Acid-base chemistry, treated *quantitatively* through equilibria, is a rich advanced (chuyên) and olympiad topic — calculating pH, understanding buffers, and interpreting titrations. It builds on equilibrium (see chemical-equilibrium-and-kinetics) applied to proton transfer in solution.

## Brønsted-Lowry Theory

- **Acid** = proton (H⁺) **donor**; **base** = proton **acceptor**. (Broader than the basic "acids give H⁺, bases give OH⁻.")
- **Conjugate pairs** — an acid becomes its conjugate base after donating H⁺ (HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻); every acid-base reaction has two conjugate pairs. A strong acid has a weak conjugate base, and vice versa.
- Water is **amphoteric** (acts as acid or base); it self-ionizes: **Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 10⁻¹⁴** at 25°C.

## Strong vs Weak

- **Strong acids/bases** (HCl, HNO₃, NaOH) — ionize **completely**; [H⁺] equals the concentration directly.
- **Weak acids/bases** (CH₃COOH, NH₃) — ionize **partially**, establishing an **equilibrium**. This is where the interesting calculations live.

## Ka, Kb & pKa

- **Ka** — acid dissociation constant: HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻, Ka = [H⁺][A⁻]/[HA]. Larger Ka = stronger acid. **Kb** likewise for bases.
- **pKa = −log Ka** — smaller pKa = stronger acid (convenient scale). **Ka·Kb = Kw** for a conjugate pair.
- For a weak acid, solve the equilibrium (ICE table) to find [H⁺] — often [H⁺] ≈ √(Ka·C) when dissociation is small.

## The pH Scale & Calculations

- **pH = −log[H⁺]**, **pOH = −log[OH⁻]**, with **pH + pOH = 14** (at 25°C). pH 7 = neutral, <7 acidic, >7 basic. Logarithmic — each unit is ×10 in [H⁺] (see exponentials-and-logarithms).
- **Calculations**: strong acid → pH from concentration directly; weak acid → via Ka equilibrium; strong base → pOH then pH. A core, heavily-tested quantitative skill.

## Buffers

Solutions that **resist pH change** — a weak acid + its conjugate base (or weak base + conjugate acid):
- They neutralize added acid or base by shifting the conjugate equilibrium (Le Chatelier).
- **Henderson-Hasselbalch equation**: **pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])** — computes buffer pH; pH ≈ pKa when acid and base are equal. Crucial in biology (blood pH) and chemistry.
- **Buffer capacity** — how much acid/base it can absorb; greatest near pKa.

## Salt Hydrolysis

Salts can make solutions acidic or basic:
- The conjugate of a **weak** acid/base **hydrolyzes** water. Salt of strong acid + weak base → acidic; weak acid + strong base → basic; strong+strong → neutral.
- Predict and calculate the pH of salt solutions via the conjugate's Ka/Kb.

## Titration Curves

Plotting pH as base (or acid) is added:
- **Equivalence point** — stoichiometrically equal acid and base. For strong-strong it's pH 7; for weak-strong it's *not* 7 (the salt hydrolyzes).
- **Buffer region** — before equivalence in a weak-acid titration, pH changes slowly (buffering); at the **half-equivalence point, pH = pKa** (a neat way to find pKa).
- **Indicators** change color near the equivalence point; choose one matching the equivalence pH.
- Interpreting titration curves (shape, equivalence, buffer regions) is a classic advanced problem.

Master advanced acid-base chemistry via **Brønsted-Lowry theory** (proton donors/acceptors, conjugate pairs, Kw), **strong vs weak** (complete vs equilibrium ionization), **Ka/Kb/pKa** (quantifying strength), **pH/pOH calculations** (strong and weak, via ICE tables), **buffers** (Henderson-Hasselbalch, resisting pH change), **salt hydrolysis**, and **titration curves** (equivalence points, pH = pKa at half-equivalence, indicators). This quantitative equilibrium treatment of protons in solution is exactly the depth that earns top marks in gifted-exam/Olympiad chemistry.
