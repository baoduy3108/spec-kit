# Kinetic Theory & Thermodynamics (Advanced Physics)

Thermal physics explains heat, temperature, and gases from two angles: the **macroscopic** (gas laws, energy, engines) and the **microscopic** (molecules in motion — kinetic theory). Connecting them is one of physics' triumphs and a rich source of gifted-exam (chuyên) and olympiad problems.

## The Ideal Gas Law

**PV = nRT** (or PV = NkT) — relating pressure, volume, amount, and temperature. It combines:
- **Boyle's law** (PV constant at fixed T), **Charles's law** (V ∝ T at fixed P), **Gay-Lussac's** (P ∝ T at fixed V).
- A cornerstone for gas problems; manipulate it across processes. Real gases deviate (van der Waals) but the ideal law is the workhorse.

## Kinetic Theory (Micro Explanation)

Kinetic theory derives gas behavior from molecules bouncing around:
- **Pressure** arises from countless molecular collisions with the walls.
- **The key result**: average translational kinetic energy per molecule = **(3/2)kT** — so **temperature *is* molecular kinetic energy**. Hotter = faster molecules. This links the microscopic to T.
- **RMS speed** v_rms = √(3kT/m) — lighter/hotter molecules move faster.
- **Degrees of freedom & equipartition** — energy distributes ½kT per degree of freedom, giving internal energy and specific heats (monatomic vs diatomic gases differ).

## Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution

Molecules don't all move at the same speed — they follow the **Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution** of speeds. It's spread out, with a peak (most probable speed) and a high-speed tail; raising temperature broadens it and shifts it faster. This statistical view explains evaporation, reaction rates (see chemical-equilibrium-and-kinetics), and why temperature is an *average*.

## First Law of Thermodynamics

**ΔU = Q − W** — internal energy change = heat added minus work done *by* the gas. Energy conservation for thermal systems:
- **Internal energy (U)** — total molecular energy; for an ideal gas depends only on temperature (U = (f/2)nRT).
- **Work** — W = ∫P dV (area under the PV curve). **Heat (Q)** — energy transferred thermally.
- Track ΔU, Q, W through any process.

## Thermodynamic Processes

Analyze gases through characteristic processes (on PV diagrams):
- **Isothermal** (constant T) — ΔU = 0, so Q = W.
- **Adiabatic** (no heat, Q = 0) — ΔU = −W; PVᵞ = constant. Fast compressions/expansions.
- **Isobaric** (constant P) — W = PΔV.
- **Isochoric** (constant V) — W = 0, so Q = ΔU.
Each has distinct P-V-T relations and energy accounting — core exam skill.

## Heat Engines & the Second Law

- **Heat engines** convert heat to work in cycles (Carnot, Otto). **Efficiency** η = W/Q_hot; the ideal **Carnot efficiency** = 1 − T_cold/T_hot sets the maximum possible.
- **Second law** — heat flows spontaneously hot→cold; no engine is 100% efficient; **entropy** (disorder) of an isolated system never decreases (see thermodynamics-and-entropy). This gives time a direction and limits all engines.

Master advanced thermal physics via the **ideal gas law** (PV = nRT and its special processes), **kinetic theory** (temperature = molecular KE, (3/2)kT, RMS speed), the **Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution** (speeds are statistical), the **first law** (ΔU = Q − W with work as PV-area), the **thermodynamic processes** (isothermal/adiabatic/isobaric/isochoric energy accounting), and **heat engines with the second law** (Carnot limit, entropy). Connecting the molecular and macroscopic pictures is exactly the depth gifted-exam/Olympiad thermal physics rewards.
