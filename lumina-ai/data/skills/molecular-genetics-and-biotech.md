# Molecular Genetics & Biotechnology (Advanced Biology)

Molecular genetics explains how genetic information in **DNA** is stored, copied, and expressed into the proteins that build and run cells — and how we now read and edit it. It's a core advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) biology topic (di truyền phân tử), building on heredity (see cell-biology-and-heredity).

## DNA Structure & Replication

- **DNA** — a double helix of nucleotides; base pairs **A-T** and **G-C** (complementary). The sequence is the genetic code.
- **Replication** — DNA copies itself **semi-conservatively**: the strands separate, and each templates a new complementary strand (via DNA polymerase). Each daughter DNA has one old, one new strand. Ensures genetic continuity across cell division.

## The Central Dogma

The flow of genetic information: **DNA → RNA → Protein**:
- **Transcription** — DNA is copied into **messenger RNA (mRNA)** (RNA polymerase reads a gene). RNA uses uracil (U) instead of thymine.
- **Translation** — ribosomes read the mRNA in **codons** (3-base groups), each specifying an amino acid (via the **genetic code**), building a protein. **tRNA** brings the matching amino acids.
- The resulting **protein** (see polymers-and-biomolecules) does the cell's work. Genes → proteins → traits.

## Gene Regulation

- Cells control **which genes are expressed when** (not all genes are active at once — that's why a liver cell differs from a neuron despite identical DNA).
- **Operons** (in bacteria, e.g. the lac operon), promoters, transcription factors, and epigenetic marks switch genes on/off. Regulation underlies development, differentiation, and response to environment.

## Mutations

- **Mutations** — changes in DNA sequence: point mutations (substitution), insertions/deletions (frameshift), chromosomal changes.
- **Effects** — silent (no change), missense (different amino acid), nonsense (premature stop), or frameshift (garbled downstream). Sources of genetic variation (fueling evolution — see evolution) but also of genetic disease and cancer.

## Biotechnology Tools

Modern tools to read and manipulate DNA:
- **PCR (polymerase chain reaction)** — amplifies tiny DNA samples into millions of copies (forensics, diagnostics, research).
- **Gel electrophoresis** — separates DNA fragments by size (DNA fingerprinting).
- **Gene cloning / recombinant DNA** — inserting genes into vectors (plasmids) and bacteria to produce proteins (insulin, vaccines).
- **CRISPR-Cas9** — precise gene **editing** — cutting and altering DNA at chosen sites; revolutionizing medicine and research (with ethical questions).
- **GMOs, gene therapy, DNA sequencing, genetic testing** — applications transforming medicine, agriculture, and biology.

## Ethics & Impact

Genetic technologies raise **ethical issues** — designer genes, privacy of genetic data, GMO safety, gene-editing embryos. A growing theme alongside the science.

Master molecular genetics via **DNA structure and semi-conservative replication**, the **central dogma** (DNA → transcription → mRNA → translation → protein, read in codons), **gene regulation** (differential expression making cells specialized), **mutations** (types and effects, source of variation and disease), and **biotechnology tools** (PCR amplification, cloning/recombinant DNA, CRISPR editing, sequencing) — with attention to **ethics**. Understanding how information flows from gene to protein, and how we now manipulate it, is the molecular depth that gifted-exam biology and modern life science demand.
