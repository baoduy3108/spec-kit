# Tactical Shooter Design

Tactical shooters (Counter-Strike, Valorant, Rainbow Six Siege) prioritize **realism, lethality, teamwork, and deliberate play** over run-and-gun action. Low time-to-kill makes every engagement lethal, no respawns raise the stakes, and victory comes from positioning, communication, and precise aim. It's the shooter genre's most methodical, competitive, and unforgiving form.

## Low TTK & Lethality

The defining trait: **you die fast**:
- **Low time-to-kill** — a couple of well-placed shots (often headshots) kill. This makes **first contact and accuracy decisive** — whoever sees/shoots first usually wins.
- Consequences: **positioning, awareness, and pre-aiming** dominate; recklessness is punished instantly; there's little room to "out-play" once caught in the open. Deliberate, careful movement (holding angles, clearing corners) is essential.
- High lethality creates intense, tense, high-stakes fights where every duel is decisive.

## Deliberate Pace & Realism

- **Slow, methodical play** — creeping, holding angles, listening for footsteps, coordinating pushes. The tension of the *approach* is as important as the fight.
- **Realism-leaning mechanics** — limited health (no regen mid-fight or slow), realistic weapon handling, sound cues that matter (footsteps, reloads). Movement often affects accuracy (can't run-and-gun accurately).
- The pace rewards patience, discipline, and mind-games over reflexes alone.

## Gunplay: Recoil & Spread Mastery

- **Skill-based shooting** — weapons have **recoil patterns** and **spread** that must be *controlled* (learned spray patterns, tap-firing, standing still for accuracy). Mastering weapon control is a deep, learnable skill and a core competitive differentiator (CS's spray control).
- **Precise, punishing gunplay** — accuracy under pressure separates players. First-shot accuracy and crosshair placement are fundamental.

## Round-Based Economy

Most tactical shooters are **round-based with an economy**:
- **Buy phase** — earn money from round outcomes/kills to buy weapons, armor, and utility each round. Managing this **economy** (full-buy vs eco/save rounds, coordinating team buys) is a strategic meta-layer as important as shooting.
- **No mid-round respawn** — die and you're out until the next round (spectating). This makes each life precious and each round a discrete high-stakes match.

## Teamwork & Communication

Tactical shooters are **fundamentally team games**:
- **Communication** — callouts (enemy positions, plans), coordinated executes, and info-sharing are essential; a communicating team beats a silent one of equal aim.
- **Coordinated tactics** — site executes, defaults, trades (avenging a fallen teammate), and role assignment (entry, support, lurk, IGL). The team's strategy and cohesion is the deepest layer.

## Map Control & Utility

- **Map control** — holding/taking key areas, chokepoints, and angles; information and positioning win rounds.
- **Utility** — grenades, smokes, flashes, and (Valorant/R6) agent abilities to block sightlines, clear angles, gather info, and enable executes. **Utility usage** is a major skill and strategic layer — smokes and flashes *create* opportunities that raw aim can't. Coordinated utility is often what wins sites.

## No-Respawn Stakes & Objectives

- **Objectives** (plant/defuse a bomb, capture, defend) structure rounds and force engagements, preventing pure camping and creating clear win conditions.
- **Elimination/round stakes** — the tension of a life that matters, clutch 1-vs-X situations, and momentum swings. The competitive drama is immense.

Design tactical shooters around **low-TTK lethality** that makes first contact decisive, a **deliberate realistic pace**, **skill-based recoil/spread gunplay**, a **round-based economy**, deep **teamwork and communication**, and **map control with utility** — all under **no-respawn stakes** and objectives. The result is a methodical, unforgiving, deeply competitive team game where positioning, precision, coordination, and smart utility — not twitchy chaos — decide the winner.
