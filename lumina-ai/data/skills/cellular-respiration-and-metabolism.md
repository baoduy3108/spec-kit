# Cellular Respiration & Metabolism (Advanced Biology)

Cellular respiration is how cells **release energy** from food (glucose) to power life — the counterpart to photosynthesis. It's a core advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) biology topic, and understanding its stages, ATP yield, and enzyme control is essential.

## The Overall Process

- **Respiration**: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy (**ATP**).
- The reverse of photosynthesis: glucose is **oxidized**, releasing its stored chemical energy, captured as ATP. Most occurs in the **mitochondria** ("powerhouse of the cell").

## ATP: The Energy Currency

- **ATP (adenosine triphosphate)** — the universal energy currency. Energy is stored in its phosphate bonds; **ATP → ADP + Pi** releases energy for cellular work (muscle contraction, active transport, synthesis).
- Cells constantly cycle ADP↔ATP; respiration's job is to regenerate ATP.

## The Three Stages

**1. Glycolysis** (in the cytoplasm)
- Glucose (6C) is split into two **pyruvate** (3C), producing a small net **2 ATP** and NADH.
- Doesn't require oxygen — the first, universal step.

**2. Krebs Cycle (Citric Acid Cycle)** (mitochondrial matrix)
- Pyruvate is further broken down, releasing **CO₂** and loading electron carriers (**NADH, FADH₂**) with high-energy electrons; a little ATP.
- Requires oxygen (aerobic).

**3. Electron Transport Chain** (inner mitochondrial membrane)
- NADH/FADH₂ deliver electrons; the energy pumps protons, and **oxidative phosphorylation** produces the bulk of ATP (~34). **Oxygen is the final electron acceptor**, forming water.
- This stage yields the most ATP — which is why oxygen is essential.

**Total** — aerobic respiration yields ~36–38 ATP per glucose.

## Aerobic vs Anaerobic

- **Aerobic** (with oxygen) — full breakdown, ~36 ATP. The efficient default.
- **Anaerobic / fermentation** (without oxygen) — only glycolysis (2 ATP), then pyruvate converts to **lactic acid** (in muscles during hard exercise) or **ethanol + CO₂** (in yeast — used in bread and alcohol). Much less energy, but fast and O₂-independent.

## Enzymes

Respiration (and all metabolism) is run by **enzymes** — protein catalysts (see chemical-equilibrium-and-kinetics for catalysis):
- **Lower activation energy**, speeding reactions enormously; specific to their substrates (lock-and-key / induced fit).
- Affected by temperature, pH (each has an optimum; denature if too extreme), and inhibitors.
- **Metabolic pathways** are enzyme-catalyzed chains; each step controlled.

## Metabolic Regulation

- **Metabolism** = all cellular reactions: **catabolism** (breaking down, releasing energy — respiration) and **anabolism** (building up, using energy — synthesis).
- **Regulation** — feedback inhibition (a product inhibits an earlier enzyme), allosteric control, and hormones tune metabolic rate to the cell's needs. Efficient, responsive energy management.

Master cellular respiration via the **overall oxidation** of glucose to release energy as **ATP** (the energy currency), the **three stages** (glycolysis in cytoplasm → Krebs cycle → electron transport chain in mitochondria, with O₂ as final electron acceptor for the bulk of ATP), **aerobic vs anaerobic** (full breakdown ~36 ATP vs fermentation's 2 ATP producing lactic acid/ethanol), the role of **enzymes** (lowering activation energy, sensitive to conditions), and **metabolic regulation** (catabolism/anabolism, feedback control). Understanding how cells extract and manage energy is the metabolic depth gifted-exam biology demands.
