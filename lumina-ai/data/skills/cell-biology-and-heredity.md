# Cell Biology & Heredity

Life is built from **cells** and continues through **heredity** — the passing of traits from parents to offspring. Cell biology explains the basic unit of life and how cells reproduce; heredity (genetics) explains how the instructions for life are stored, copied, and inherited. Together they underpin all biology.

## The Cell: Life's Basic Unit

All living things are made of **cells** (cell theory: cells are the basic unit of life, all cells come from pre-existing cells):
- **Prokaryotic cells** — simple, no nucleus (bacteria, archaea); DNA floats free.
- **Eukaryotic cells** — complex, with a nucleus and membrane-bound **organelles** (plants, animals, fungi, protists).

Key **organelles** (in eukaryotes):
- **Nucleus** — holds DNA, the control center.
- **Mitochondria** — produce energy (ATP) via respiration ("powerhouse").
- **Ribosomes** — build proteins. **Endoplasmic reticulum & Golgi** — make, process, and ship molecules.
- **Cell membrane** — controls what enters/exits. **Cell wall & chloroplasts** (plants) — structure and photosynthesis.

Each organelle has a specialized job; the cell is a coordinated micro-factory.

## Cell Division

Cells reproduce by dividing:
- **Mitosis** — one cell makes two *identical* daughter cells (same chromosomes). Used for growth, repair, and asexual reproduction. DNA is copied, then split evenly.
- **Meiosis** — makes **gametes** (sex cells: eggs, sperm) with *half* the chromosomes, and shuffles genes (crossing over) creating genetic variation. Fertilization then combines two halves into a new full set. This is the basis of sexual reproduction and why offspring differ from parents.

## DNA & Chromosomes

- **DNA** — the molecule storing genetic instructions, a double helix of base pairs (A-T, G-C). Its sequence is the code (see molecular-biology-and-gene-expression for how it's read).
- **Genes** — segments of DNA coding for traits/proteins.
- **Chromosomes** — DNA packaged into structures; humans have 23 pairs (one of each from each parent). This organization lets DNA be copied and distributed during division.

## Mendelian Genetics

Gregor Mendel discovered the rules of **inheritance** with pea plants:
- **Genes and alleles** — each gene has variant forms (**alleles**); offspring inherit one allele from each parent for each gene.
- **Dominant vs recessive** — a **dominant** allele masks a **recessive** one. You show the dominant trait if you have at least one dominant allele; the recessive trait only appears with two recessive alleles.
- **Genotype vs phenotype** — genotype is the genetic makeup (e.g. Bb); phenotype is the observable trait (e.g. brown eyes).
- **Punnett squares** — predict offspring trait ratios from parents' alleles (a heterozygous cross gives the classic 3:1 dominant:recessive ratio).

## How Traits Pass On

The synthesis: **DNA (genes) in chromosomes carries the instructions; meiosis and fertilization pass one set from each parent to offspring; dominant/recessive rules determine which traits show.** This explains family resemblance, genetic variation, why some traits skip generations, and the inheritance of genetic conditions. (Real inheritance is often more complex — multiple genes, incomplete dominance, environment — but Mendel's rules are the foundation.)

Reason about life's units and continuity via **cell structure** (organelles as specialized machinery; prokaryote vs eukaryote), **cell division** (mitosis for identical copies, meiosis for varied gametes), **DNA/genes/chromosomes** (the stored instructions), and **Mendelian genetics** (alleles, dominant/recessive, genotype/phenotype, Punnett squares) to predict how traits pass from parents to offspring. Cells build life; heredity carries it forward.
