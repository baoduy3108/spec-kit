# Analytical Chemistry (Hóa phân tích)

Analytical chemistry answers **"what is it?"** (qualitative) and **"how much?"** (quantitative) — identifying and measuring the components of a sample. It's a precise, calculation-heavy advanced/chuyên topic underpinning quality control, forensics, medicine, and research.

## Qualitative vs Quantitative Analysis

- **Qualitative** — *identifying* what substances/ions are present (using characteristic reactions, precipitates, colors, flame tests — see inorganic-chemistry-and-periodicity).
- **Quantitative** — *measuring how much* is present (concentration, mass, percentage). The bulk of analytical technique.

## Titrations (The Core Technique)

Determining an unknown concentration by reacting it with a **standard solution** of known concentration until the **equivalence point**:
- **Acid-base titration** — neutralization; use an indicator (or pH meter) to detect the endpoint; calculate via mole ratios (see acid-base-and-ph-equilibria, stoichiometry). Titration curves reveal pKa and equivalence.
- **Redox titration** — oxidation-reduction reactions (e.g. KMnO₄, iodometry — see electrochemistry-and-redox); often self-indicating.
- **Complexometric titration** — metal ions with chelating agents like EDTA (see transition-metals-and-coordination), for water hardness and metal analysis.
- **Precipitation titration** — forming an insoluble product (argentometry for chlorides).
- **Key calculation**: at equivalence, moles(titrant) × ratio = moles(analyte); back-calculate concentration. Standardization of solutions and back-titration are common.

## Gravimetric Analysis

- Determining amount by **mass** — precipitate the analyte as a pure, insoluble compound, filter, dry, and weigh; calculate from stoichiometry. Highly accurate but slow. Uses the solubility product (see solutions-and-colligative-properties).

## Instrumental Methods

Modern analysis relies on instruments:
- **Spectroscopy** — measuring how a sample absorbs/emits light: **UV-Vis** (concentration via Beer's law, A = εcl — absorbance ∝ concentration), **IR** (functional groups), **atomic absorption** (metals), **NMR/mass spec** (structure). Based on light-matter interaction (see optics/electromagnetism).
- **Chromatography** — separating mixture components by differential movement through a medium: **gas (GC)** and **liquid (HPLC)** chromatography; components identified/quantified by retention time and detector response.
- **Electroanalytical** methods (potentiometry — pH meters, ion-selective electrodes).

## Calibration & Standards

- **Calibration curve** — measure the instrument response for known-concentration **standards**, plot response vs concentration (linear, per Beer's law), then read the unknown's concentration from its response. Fundamental to quantitative instrumental analysis.
- **Standard solutions** — accurately known concentrations (primary standards); serial dilution.

## Error, Precision & Accuracy

Rigor about measurement quality (see scientific-method-and-experimentation):
- **Accuracy** (closeness to true value) vs **precision** (reproducibility/consistency).
- **Errors** — systematic (bias, consistent — from miscalibration) vs random (scatter). Minimize both.
- **Significant figures**, uncertainty propagation, and statistics (mean, standard deviation — see statistics-fundamentals) to report results honestly with confidence.

Master analytical chemistry via **qualitative vs quantitative** analysis, the central **titration methods** (acid-base, redox, complexometric, precipitation — with equivalence-point mole calculations), **gravimetric analysis** (mass via precipitation), **instrumental methods** (spectroscopy with Beer's law, chromatography for separation), **calibration curves** (quantifying unknowns against standards), and rigorous handling of **error, precision, and accuracy**. Precise identification and measurement — turning a sample into reliable numbers — is the analytical skill that hóa phân tích and lab science demand.
