# Electrochemistry & Redox (Advanced Chemistry)

Electrochemistry connects chemical reactions and electricity — reactions that transfer electrons (**redox**) can generate current (batteries) or be driven by it (electrolysis). It's a quantitative, high-yield topic in advanced (chuyên) and olympiad chemistry, combining redox balancing with potentials and Faraday's laws.

## Oxidation-Reduction (Redox)

Redox reactions involve **electron transfer**:
- **Oxidation** = loss of electrons (oxidation state increases); **Reduction** = gain of electrons (oxidation state decreases). Mnemonic: **OIL RIG** (Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain).
- **Oxidizing agent** — gets reduced (takes electrons); **reducing agent** — gets oxidized (gives electrons). They pair up.
- **Oxidation states** — assign them by rules (O usually −2, H +1, etc.) to track electron flow and identify what's oxidized/reduced.

## Balancing Redox Reactions

A core skill — the **half-reaction method**:
1. Split into oxidation and reduction **half-reactions**.
2. Balance atoms (add H₂O for O, H⁺ for H in acidic solution; OH⁻ in basic).
3. Balance charge by adding **electrons**.
4. Scale so electrons lost = electrons gained, then add.
Ensures both mass and charge conservation — heavily tested.

## Galvanic (Voltaic) Cells

A spontaneous redox reaction generating electricity:
- **Two half-cells** — oxidation at the **anode** (negative), reduction at the **cathode** (positive); electrons flow through the external wire anode→cathode; ions flow through a **salt bridge**.
- **Cell notation**: anode | anode solution || cathode solution | cathode.
- Chemical energy → electrical energy. This is how **batteries** work.

## Electrode Potentials & EMF

Quantifying the driving force:
- **Standard electrode potential (E°)** — each half-reaction's tendency to be reduced, measured against the standard hydrogen electrode (0.00 V). Tabulated as reduction potentials.
- **Cell EMF**: E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode. **Positive E°cell → spontaneous** reaction.
- **The electrochemical series** ranks metals/half-reactions by E°; higher (more positive) = stronger oxidizer/more easily reduced. Predicts which metal displaces another, corrosion, and reactivity.
- **Nernst equation** — corrects E for non-standard concentrations; links E° to the equilibrium constant K and ΔG (ΔG = −nFE°).

## Electrolysis & Faraday's Laws

The reverse — using electricity to *drive* a non-spontaneous reaction:
- **Electrolysis** — current forces oxidation/reduction (splitting water, extracting/refining metals, electroplating). Here anode is +, cathode is −.
- **Faraday's laws** — the mass deposited/liberated is proportional to charge passed: **m = (Q·M)/(n·F)**, where Q = It (current × time), F = 96,500 C/mol (Faraday constant), n = electrons per ion. Quantitative electrolysis calculations are a classic exam problem type.

## Applications

- **Batteries and fuel cells** (portable energy), **electroplating** (coating metals), **metal extraction/refining** (aluminum, copper), **corrosion** and its prevention (galvanizing, sacrificial anodes). Electrochemistry underlies enormous practical technology.

Master advanced electrochemistry via **redox fundamentals** (oxidation states, OIL RIG, agents), **half-reaction balancing** (mass and charge), **galvanic cells** (anode/cathode, electron/ion flow), **electrode potentials and EMF** (E°cell, the electrochemical series, Nernst, link to ΔG/K), and **electrolysis with Faraday's laws** (m = QM/nF quantitative calculations). This blend of conceptual redox and quantitative potential/charge analysis is exactly the depth gifted-exam/Olympiad chemistry demands.
