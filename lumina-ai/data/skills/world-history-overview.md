# World History Overview

World history is the story of human societies changing over time — how civilizations rose, interacted, and transformed. This overview maps the major eras and turning points, and (as important) *how to think historically*: in terms of causes, consequences, context, and continuity vs change.

## Early Civilizations (c. 3500–500 BCE)

- **The Neolithic Revolution** — agriculture (~10,000 BCE) let humans settle, produce surplus, and build cities — the foundation of civilization.
- **River-valley civilizations** — Mesopotamia (writing, law — Hammurabi), Egypt (pharaohs, pyramids), Indus Valley, and China (Yellow River). Writing, cities, states, and organized religion emerge.

## Classical Era (c. 800 BCE–500 CE)

- **Greece** — democracy, philosophy (Socrates, Plato, Aristotle), science, art.
- **Rome** — republic then empire; law, engineering, and administration spanning the Mediterranean.
- **Empires and thought in Asia** — Persia, Maurya/Gupta India (Buddhism, Hinduism spread), Han China (Confucianism, the Silk Road). Major religions and philosophies that still shape the world take form.

## The Medieval World (c. 500–1500)

- After Rome's fall: **feudal Europe**, the **Byzantine Empire**, and the rise of **Islam** (a vast caliphate advancing science, math, medicine).
- **Trade networks** (Silk Road, Indian Ocean) connected Afro-Eurasia; the **Mongol Empire** briefly unified much of it.
- Powerful states across Asia, Africa (Mali, Great Zimbabwe), and the Americas (Maya, Aztec, Inca).

## Early Modern & Age of Exploration (c. 1450–1750)

- **The Renaissance** — revival of art, learning, and humanism in Europe.
- **Exploration and the Columbian Exchange** — European voyages linked the hemispheres, transferring crops, people, and disease — and began **colonialism** and the **transatlantic slave trade**, reshaping the globe (with immense human cost).
- **The Scientific Revolution** — Copernicus, Galileo, Newton established modern science and the empirical method.

## Revolutions (c. 1750–1900)

- **The Enlightenment** — reason, liberty, rights (Locke, Rousseau) — fueling political change.
- **Political revolutions** — American and French Revolutions spread ideas of democracy, rights, and nationalism.
- **The Industrial Revolution** — machines, factories, and fossil fuels transformed economies and societies (urbanization, capitalism, new class structures), while **imperialism** carved up Africa and Asia.

## The 20th Century

- **World War I** (1914–18) — industrial-scale war reshaped borders and empires.
- **The interwar era** — the Great Depression, the rise of fascism and communism.
- **World War II** (1939–45) — the deadliest conflict; the Holocaust; atomic weapons; a new global order.
- **The Cold War** (1945–91) — US–USSR rivalry, decolonization freeing dozens of nations (including Vietnam), proxy wars, the nuclear age, ending with the Soviet collapse.

## The Modern Era

- **Globalization** — deepening economic and cultural interconnection; the digital and information revolution.
- **Ongoing themes** — technological acceleration, human rights movements, environmental challenges (climate change), and shifting global powers.

## Thinking Historically

History isn't just dates — it's **analysis**:
- **Cause and consequence** — why events happened and what followed.
- **Context** — understanding the past on its own terms, not by present standards.
- **Continuity and change** — what persisted vs transformed.
- **Sources and perspective** — history is interpreted from evidence, and accounts carry viewpoints (and bias).

Grasp world history as a sweep from **early civilizations** → **classical empires and enduring religions/philosophies** → the **medieval interconnected world** → **exploration, colonialism, and the Scientific Revolution** → the **Enlightenment, political and industrial revolutions** → the **World Wars and Cold War** → the **globalized modern era** — always thinking in terms of **causes, consequences, context, and change**. History explains how today's world came to be.
