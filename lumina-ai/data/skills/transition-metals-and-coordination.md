# Transition Metals & Coordination Chemistry (Advanced Chemistry)

Transition metals (the d-block) have distinctive properties — variable oxidation states, coloured compounds, catalytic activity, and the ability to form **complex ions**. Coordination chemistry, the study of these complexes, is an advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) topic (phức chất) rich in structure and colour.

## Transition Metal Properties

The d-block elements (Sc–Zn and below) share features from their partially-filled **d orbitals**:
- **Variable oxidation states** — they can lose different numbers of electrons (Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺, Cu⁺/Cu²⁺, Mn from +2 to +7), because d and s electrons are close in energy. Central to their redox chemistry.
- **Coloured ions/compounds** — due to d-orbital electron transitions (below).
- **Catalytic activity** — variable oxidation states let them shuttle electrons (industrial catalysts, enzymes).
- **Paramagnetism** — unpaired d electrons; **high density, hardness, high melting points**.

## Complex Ions & Ligands

- A **complex ion** — a central metal ion bonded to surrounding **ligands** (molecules/ions that donate electron pairs — Lewis bases). E.g. [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺, [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻.
- **Ligands** — H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻, CN⁻, OH⁻; **coordinate (dative) bonds** where the ligand donates both electrons to the metal.
- **Monodentate** (one bond, like NH₃) vs **polydentate/chelating** (multiple bonds, like EDTA) ligands.

## Coordination Number & Geometry

- **Coordination number** — the number of ligand bonds to the metal (commonly 4 or 6).
- **Geometry** — 6 → **octahedral**; 4 → **tetrahedral** or **square planar** (depends on the metal). Determines the complex's shape and properties.
- **Isomerism** — geometric (cis/trans) and optical isomers in complexes.

## Colour & the Spectrochemical Series

The signature feature:
- In a complex, ligands split the d orbitals into two energy levels (**crystal field splitting**, Δ). Electrons absorb visible light to jump the gap; the **complementary colour** is transmitted — hence the vivid colours.
- **The spectrochemical series** ranks ligands by how much they split the d orbitals (I⁻ < Cl⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < CN⁻). Stronger-field ligands → larger Δ → different colour absorbed.
- Colour depends on the metal, its oxidation state, and the ligands — explaining why [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is blue but [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ is deep blue-violet.

## Stability & Reactions

- **Stability constants** — complexes form in equilibrium; larger constants = more stable. **Chelate effect** — polydentate ligands form especially stable complexes (entropy-favoured).
- **Ligand exchange** — one ligand can replace another (shifting colour), a common reaction/observation.
- **Applications** — catalysis, biological complexes (haemoglobin's iron, chlorophyll's magnesium), analysis, and extraction.

Master transition-metal chemistry via the **d-block properties** (variable oxidation states, colour, catalysis from partially-filled d orbitals), **complex ions and ligands** (dative bonds, mono/polydentate), **coordination number and geometry** (octahedral, tetrahedral, square planar), **colour and the spectrochemical series** (d-orbital splitting absorbing visible light), and **stability** (stability constants, the chelate effect, ligand exchange). The interplay of metal, oxidation state, and ligand — producing structure and vivid colour — is the fascinating depth of gifted-exam coordination chemistry.
