# Advanced Solid Geometry (Hình học không gian)

Solid geometry reasons about **3D figures** — lines, planes, and solids in space. It's a demanding THPT and thi-chuyên topic requiring strong spatial visualization, plus two complementary approaches: **synthetic** (pure geometric reasoning) and the **coordinate method** (see coordinate-and-vector-geometry).

## Lines & Planes in Space

The foundation — relationships between lines and planes:
- **Lines** — can be parallel, intersecting, or **skew** (non-parallel, non-intersecting — unique to 3D).
- **A plane** is determined by 3 non-collinear points, a line + external point, or two intersecting/parallel lines.
- **Parallelism and perpendicularity** — a line ⊥ a plane if it's ⊥ to two intersecting lines in it; two planes are ⊥ if one contains a line ⊥ to the other.
- **The theorem of three perpendiculars** and projections onto planes are key tools.

## Angles & Distances (Core Skills)

The most-tested computations:
- **Angles** — between two lines, a line and a plane (angle with its projection), and two planes (**dihedral angle** — measured perpendicular to the edge). Finding these requires careful construction or vectors.
- **Distances** — point to line, point to plane, between two skew lines (the hardest — the common perpendicular), line to parallel plane. These are staple exam problems, often solved most reliably by coordinates.

## Polyhedra & Solids

- **Prisms** — two parallel congruent bases joined by parallelograms (cuboid is a right rectangular prism).
- **Pyramids** — a base + apex; the tetrahedron (triangular pyramid) is fundamental.
- **Cylinders, cones, spheres** — solids of revolution.
- **Regular polyhedra** (Platonic solids) and their symmetry.
- **Euler's formula** — for polyhedra, V − E + F = 2 (vertices − edges + faces).

## Volumes & Surface Areas

Key formulas (frequently required):
- **Prism/cylinder**: V = base area × height.
- **Pyramid/cone**: V = ⅓ × base area × height.
- **Sphere**: V = 4/3·πr³, surface = 4πr².
- **Cone/cylinder** lateral surfaces, and composite/derived solids.
- **Ratios** — volumes of similar solids scale as the cube of the linear ratio (a key exam trick).

## Cross-Sections (Thiết diện)

- Finding the **cross-section** where a plane cuts a solid — a classic, spatially-demanding problem. Determine the intersection polygon by finding where the cutting plane meets each face/edge (using parallelism and intersection lines). Requires strong visualization.

## The Coordinate Method in Space

Often the most reliable approach for hard problems:
- **Set up axes** cleverly (put a vertex at the origin, edges along axes) and assign coordinates to all points.
- **Vectors** — use the **dot product** for angles/perpendicularity and **cross product** for normals, areas, and volumes (scalar triple product gives tetrahedron/parallelepiped volume).
- **Planes** — ax + by + cz + d = 0 with normal (a,b,c); compute distances and angles by formula.
- This converts spatial reasoning into algebra — bypassing tricky constructions. Knowing when to switch from synthetic to coordinates is the key strategic skill.

Master solid geometry via **lines and planes in space** (parallel/skew/perpendicular relationships, three-perpendiculars theorem), computing **angles and distances** (line-plane, dihedral, skew-line distance), **polyhedra and solids** (prisms, pyramids, spheres, Euler's formula), **volumes and surface areas** (with the cube-ratio for similar solids), **cross-sections**, and the **coordinate method** (axes + vectors for a reliable algebraic solution). Balancing spatial visualization with coordinate-bashing — and choosing the right one — is what conquers thi-chuyên hình học không gian.
