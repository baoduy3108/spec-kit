# Electron Configuration & Quantum Numbers (Advanced Chemistry)

*How* electrons arrange around the nucleus determines an atom's entire chemistry. Beyond the simple "shells" model (see atomic-structure-and-periodic-table), advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) chemistry uses **orbitals** and **quantum numbers** to explain the periodic table's very structure and predict chemical behaviour.

## Orbitals

Electrons occupy **orbitals** — regions of probability where an electron is likely found (not fixed paths):
- **Types** — **s** (spherical, 1 orbital), **p** (dumbbell, 3 orbitals), **d** (5 orbitals), **f** (7 orbitals). Each orbital holds a maximum of **2 electrons**.
- **Subshells and shells** — orbitals group into subshells (s, p, d, f) within shells (energy levels n). Shell n holds 2n² electrons.
- Orbitals explain bonding geometry, hybridization, and (for d orbitals) transition-metal colour and complexes.

## Quantum Numbers

Four numbers uniquely specify each electron's state:
- **Principal (n)** — the shell/energy level (1, 2, 3...); bigger n = higher energy, farther out.
- **Azimuthal (l)** — the subshell/orbital shape (0=s, 1=p, 2=d, 3=f).
- **Magnetic (mₗ)** — the specific orbital orientation within a subshell.
- **Spin (mₛ)** — the electron's spin (+½ or −½).
No two electrons in an atom share all four (Pauli — below).

## The Three Rules for Filling

Electron configuration follows three principles:
- **Aufbau principle** — electrons fill the **lowest-energy** orbitals first (1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d... — note 4s fills before 3d due to energy overlap).
- **Pauli exclusion principle** — an orbital holds at most 2 electrons, with **opposite spins** (no two electrons share all four quantum numbers).
- **Hund's rule** — within a subshell, electrons occupy orbitals **singly first** (with parallel spins) before pairing — minimizing repulsion.

## Writing Configurations

- **Notation** — 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s²... listing subshells and electron counts. E.g. oxygen = 1s² 2s² 2p⁴.
- **Noble-gas shorthand** — [Ne] 3s² 3p⁴ for sulfur.
- **Orbital diagrams** — boxes with arrows showing spins (applying Hund's rule).
- **Exceptions** — Cr and Cu ([Ar]3d⁵4s¹, 3d¹⁰4s¹) — half/fully-filled d subshells are extra stable.

## Configuration Explains Periodicity

The deep payoff — configuration *is* the reason for the periodic table's structure:
- **Blocks** — the s, p, d, f blocks correspond to which subshell is being filled. Period length matches subshell capacities.
- **Valence electrons** (outermost) determine chemical behaviour; elements in a group have the same valence configuration → similar chemistry.
- **Trends** (ionization energy, atomic radius — see inorganic-chemistry-and-periodicity) follow from how tightly configured electrons are held.
- **Ion formation** — atoms gain/lose electrons to reach stable (noble-gas or half/full-subshell) configurations.

Master electron configuration via **orbitals** (s/p/d/f shapes and capacities, 2 electrons each), the **four quantum numbers** (n, l, mₗ, mₛ specifying each electron), and the **three filling rules** — **Aufbau** (lowest energy first), **Pauli** (2 per orbital, opposite spins), and **Hund's** (singly fill before pairing) — to write configurations, and understand that **configuration explains the periodic table's blocks, groups, trends, and chemistry**. This orbital-level picture of the atom is the structural foundation beneath all of gifted-exam chemistry.
