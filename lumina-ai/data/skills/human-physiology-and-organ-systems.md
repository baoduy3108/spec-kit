# Human Physiology & Organ Systems (Advanced Biology)

Physiology explains **how the body works** — how organ systems perform life's functions and coordinate to keep the internal environment stable. It's an advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) biology topic connecting anatomy to function, unified by the principle of **homeostasis**.

## Levels of Organization

- **Cells → tissues → organs → organ systems → organism.** Specialized cells form tissues (epithelial, connective, muscle, nervous), which build organs, which cooperate in systems.

## Major Organ Systems

- **Circulatory** — the heart pumps blood through vessels, transporting oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and wastes. Blood (red cells, white cells, plasma, platelets); the double circuit (to lungs and to body).
- **Respiratory** — lungs exchange gases: oxygen in, CO₂ out, across alveoli into blood. Breathing driven by the diaphragm.
- **Digestive** — breaks food into absorbable nutrients (mechanical + enzymatic — see stoichiometry of biomolecules), absorbs them (small intestine), eliminates waste.
- **Excretory (urinary)** — kidneys filter blood, removing wastes (urea) and regulating water/salt balance, producing urine.
- **Nervous & endocrine** — control and coordinate (see nervous-and-endocrine-systems).
- **Musculoskeletal** — bones and muscles for support and movement (see statics/mechanics for the physics).
- **Immune** — defends against pathogens (see immunology-and-disease).
- **Reproductive** — produces offspring.

## Homeostasis (The Unifying Principle)

The body maintains a **stable internal environment** despite external changes:
- **Homeostasis** — keeping variables (temperature, blood glucose, pH, water, ion balance) within narrow ranges essential for life.
- Regulated variables: body temperature (~37°C), blood pH (~7.4), glucose, osmolarity, oxygen/CO₂.
- When conditions drift, systems respond to correct them — the essence of physiology.

## Feedback Mechanisms

Homeostasis works through **feedback loops** (see control-systems-and-pid for the general principle):
- **Negative feedback** (the main mechanism) — a change triggers a response that *reverses* it, restoring balance. Examples: temperature regulation (sweating/shivering), blood glucose (insulin lowers it, glucagon raises it), blood pressure. Self-correcting, stabilizing.
- **Positive feedback** (rarer) — a change amplifies itself to a rapid completion (childbirth contractions, blood clotting).
- The **receptor → control center → effector** loop is the physiological pattern.

## System Integration

The body is an **integrated whole**, not isolated parts:
- Systems cooperate: the respiratory and circulatory systems together deliver oxygen; the digestive, circulatory, and excretory systems together manage nutrients and wastes; the nervous and endocrine systems coordinate all others.
- **Example** — exercise: muscles demand more oxygen → nervous system speeds the heart and breathing → circulatory delivers O₂ and removes CO₂/heat → excretory and skin manage water/temperature. All coordinated for one goal.

Master human physiology via the **organ systems** (circulatory, respiratory, digestive, excretory, nervous, endocrine, immune, musculoskeletal — each with its function), the unifying principle of **homeostasis** (stable internal environment), **feedback mechanisms** (negative feedback self-correcting via receptor-control-effector loops), and **system integration** (organs cooperating as a coordinated whole). Understanding the body as an integrated, self-regulating system is the physiological depth that gifted-exam human biology rewards.
