# Fluid Mechanics & Pressure (Advanced Physics)

Fluid mechanics studies liquids and gases at rest (statics) and in motion (dynamics) — pressure, buoyancy, and flow. It's an advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) and olympiad topic (cơ học chất lưu) explaining why things float, how hydraulics work, and why airplanes fly.

## Pressure & Pascal's Principle

- **Pressure P = F/A** — force per unit area (Pascals). The same force over a smaller area gives higher pressure.
- **Pascal's principle** — pressure applied to an enclosed fluid transmits **equally** in all directions. This is the basis of **hydraulics**: a small force on a small piston creates the same pressure that exerts a large force on a large piston (F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂) — force multiplication in car lifts and brakes.

## Hydrostatic Pressure

- Pressure in a fluid **increases with depth**: **P = P₀ + ρgh** (ρ = density, g = gravity, h = depth). Deeper = more fluid weight above = more pressure.
- Independent of container shape (depends only on depth) — the hydrostatic paradox.
- **Pressure gauges, barometers, and manometers** apply this. Atmospheric pressure is the weight of air above.

## Buoyancy & Archimedes' Principle

- **Archimedes' principle** — a submerged/floating object experiences an upward **buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces**: F_b = ρ_fluid·V_displaced·g.
- **Floating vs sinking** — an object floats if its average density < the fluid's (buoyancy ≥ weight); sinks if denser. A floating object displaces its own weight in fluid.
- Explains ships, submarines, balloons, and why ice floats. A classic problem type.

## Fluid Dynamics: Continuity

- **Continuity equation** — for incompressible flow, **A₁v₁ = A₂v₂** (flow rate is constant). A narrower pipe → faster flow (why a thumb on a hose speeds the water). Conservation of mass for fluids.

## Bernoulli's Principle

- **Bernoulli's equation** — along a streamline, **P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant** (a statement of energy conservation for flowing fluid: pressure + kinetic + potential energy per volume).
- **Key consequence** — where a fluid flows **faster**, its **pressure is lower**. This explains lift on airplane wings (faster air over the curved top → lower pressure → lift), the curve of a spinning ball, atomizers, and Venturi meters.
- A powerful, sometimes counterintuitive principle, heavily featured in advanced problems.

## Applications & Real Fluids

- **Hydraulics** (Pascal), **flotation and density measurement** (Archimedes), **flow and lift** (continuity + Bernoulli).
- **Viscosity and turbulence** — real fluids have internal friction (viscosity) and can flow smoothly (laminar) or chaotically (turbulent); the idealized equations assume non-viscous, laminar flow.

Master fluid mechanics via **pressure and Pascal's principle** (P = F/A, hydraulic force multiplication), **hydrostatic pressure** (P = P₀ + ρgh, increasing with depth), **buoyancy** (Archimedes: upward force = weight of displaced fluid, floating vs sinking by density), the **continuity equation** (A₁v₁ = A₂v₂, mass conservation), and **Bernoulli's principle** (P + ½ρv² + ρgh constant; faster flow = lower pressure, explaining lift). These connect pressure, energy, and flow — exactly the depth gifted-exam/olympiad fluid problems require.
