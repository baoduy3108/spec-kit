# Advanced English Grammar & Usage

Beyond basic English (see writing-well-in-english), advanced grammar covers the precise structures tested in gifted-exam English (chuyên Anh) and proficiency exams (IELTS, TOEFL). Mastering tenses, conditionals, clauses, and the tricky points separates fluent, accurate English from merely functional English.

## Verb Tenses & Aspect

The English tense-aspect system is intricate:
- **12 tenses** — combining time (past/present/future) with aspect (simple/continuous/perfect/perfect-continuous).
- **Key contrasts** — present perfect ("I have lived here for years", connecting past to now) vs simple past ("I lived there in 2010", finished); past perfect for earlier-past ("I had left before she arrived"); continuous for ongoing action.
- **Aspect** conveys *how* an action relates to time (completed, ongoing, repeated) — a common source of error.

## Conditionals

"If" structures expressing conditions and hypotheticals:
- **Zero** (general truth: if you heat ice, it melts), **first** (real future: if it rains, I'll stay), **second** (unreal present: if I were rich, I'd travel), **third** (unreal past: if I had known, I would have come).
- **Mixed conditionals** and inverted forms (Had I known...). Understanding real vs hypothetical and the tense backshift is essential.

## The Subjunctive & Modals

- **Subjunctive** — for wishes, demands, hypotheticals ("I suggest he *go*"; "If I *were* you"; "It's essential that she *be* present"). Subtle in English but tested.
- **Modal verbs** — can/could, may/might, must, should, would — expressing possibility, obligation, deduction, and their perfect forms ("must have been", "should have gone") for past speculation.

## Clauses

- **Relative clauses** — who/which/that/whose/where; **defining** (essential, no commas) vs **non-defining** (extra info, commas). Reduced relative clauses.
- **Noun clauses, adverbial clauses**, and clause reduction (participle phrases) for sophisticated sentences.

## Passive & Reported Speech

- **Passive voice** — formed with be + past participle; used when the actor is unknown/unimportant (and in formal/academic register — though overuse weakens prose).
- **Reported (indirect) speech** — backshifting tenses and changing pronouns/time words when reporting what someone said ("She said she *was* tired").

## Articles, Prepositions & Tricky Points

- **Articles (a/an/the/zero)** — a persistent challenge (definiteness, generic vs specific, uncountables). The top marker of non-native usage.
- **Prepositions** — idiomatic and unpredictable (depend *on*, good *at*, interested *in*).
- **Inversion** — for emphasis/formality ("Never have I seen...", "Not only did he..."). A hallmark of advanced usage.
- **Subject-verb agreement** with tricky subjects, **countable/uncountable** nouns, **gerund vs infinitive** after verbs (enjoy doing, want to do).

## Exam Strategy

- Common exam pitfalls: tense consistency, article errors, preposition collocations, conditionals, and subject-verb agreement.
- **Collocations and natural usage** (see writing-well-in-english) matter as much as rules — exams reward idiomatic, precise English.

Master advanced English via the **tense-aspect system** (perfect vs simple, continuous), **conditionals** (real vs hypothetical, the four types + mixed), the **subjunctive and modals** (including perfect modals for past speculation), **clauses** (defining/non-defining relatives, reductions), **passive and reported speech** (backshifting), and the **tricky points** (articles, prepositions, inversion, agreement, gerund/infinitive). Precision in these structures — plus natural collocation — is what earns top marks in chuyên Anh and proficiency exams.
