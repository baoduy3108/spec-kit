# Optics & Light (THPT Physics)

Optics studies **how light behaves** — how it travels, reflects, bends, and forms images. It's a THPT physics topic bridging ray behavior (geometric optics: lenses, mirrors) and light's wave nature (interference, diffraction). Light is both an electromagnetic wave and, remarkably, exhibits particle behavior (see below).

## Reflection & Refraction

Light traveling in straight rays behaves predictably at surfaces:
- **Reflection** — light bounces off a surface; the **angle of incidence = angle of reflection** (measured from the normal). Mirrors work this way.
- **Refraction** — light **bends** when passing between media of different optical density (air→water), because its speed changes. This is why a straw looks bent in water.

## Snell's Law & Total Internal Reflection

- **Snell's law** — n₁·sin θ₁ = n₂·sin θ₂, where **n** is the **refractive index** (how much a medium slows light; n = c/v). Light bends toward the normal entering a denser medium, away leaving it.
- **Total internal reflection** — beyond a **critical angle**, light going from dense to less-dense medium reflects *entirely* internally (doesn't refract out). This is how **optical fibers** carry light/data and why diamonds sparkle. A key THPT concept.

## Lenses & Mirrors

Curved surfaces focus light to form **images**:
- **Converging (convex) lens / concave mirror** — bring parallel rays to a **focal point**; can form real, inverted images (cameras, eyes, projectors) or magnified virtual images (magnifying glass).
- **Diverging (concave) lens / convex mirror** — spread rays; form smaller, virtual, upright images (peepholes, car mirrors).
- **Lens/mirror equation**: 1/f = 1/do + 1/di (focal length, object and image distances); **magnification** m = −di/do. **Ray diagrams** trace principal rays to locate images (a core exam skill).
- **Real vs virtual images**, upright vs inverted, magnified vs reduced — determined by object position relative to the focal point.

## The Wave Nature of Light

Light is a wave (an electromagnetic wave — see electromagnetism), shown by:
- **Interference** — overlapping light waves reinforce or cancel (Young's double-slit produces bright/dark fringes — decisive evidence light is a wave; see oscillations-and-waves).
- **Diffraction** — light bends around edges/through narrow slits, spreading out. Both interference and diffraction reveal wave behavior invisible in ray optics.
- These are heavily tested in THPT (giao thoa ánh sáng — fringe spacing calculations).

## The Electromagnetic Spectrum

- Visible light is a tiny band of the **electromagnetic spectrum**: radio → microwave → infrared → **visible (red→violet)** → ultraviolet → X-ray → gamma. All are EM waves differing in wavelength/frequency.
- Shorter wavelength = higher frequency = higher energy (E = hf — the photon energy, hinting at light's particle nature: the **photon**, key to the photoelectric effect and quantum physics).

## Color & Dispersion

- **Color** corresponds to wavelength (red ~700nm long, violet ~400nm short).
- **Dispersion** — a prism (or raindrops) separates white light into a spectrum because refractive index varies slightly with wavelength (violet bends most). This makes rainbows and reveals white light is a mixture of colors.

Master THPT optics via **reflection and refraction** (angle laws, bending between media), **Snell's law and total internal reflection** (refractive index, critical angle, fiber optics), **lenses and mirrors** (focal points, image formation, ray diagrams, the 1/f equation), the **wave nature of light** (interference and diffraction proving light is a wave), the **EM spectrum** (light as one band; photon energy), and **dispersion/color**. Light behaves as rays for imaging and as waves for interference — mastering both explains the exam's optics problems.
