# CRPG & Choice-Driven Design

Western/computer RPGs (Baldur's Gate, Fallout, Disco Elysium, The Witcher) emphasize **player agency, roleplay, and consequence** — *you* shape who your character is and how the story unfolds. Where JRPGs (see jrpg-design-and-conventions) tell an authored tale, CRPGs hand the player authorship: build your character, make hard choices, and watch the world react. The design challenge is a reactive, branching experience.

## Player-Authored Character & Roleplay

The foundation is **you define the protagonist**:
- **Character creation** — build stats, skills, background, class, and often appearance/personality. A blank-or-flexible slate you inhabit, not a fixed hero.
- **Roleplay** — you decide *who your character is* — moral alignment, personality, approach (diplomat, brute, sneak, mage). The game supports playing *your* way.
- **Build variety** — different character builds enable different solutions and playstyles (see rpg-systems-and-progression). Your build shapes the experience.

## Meaningful Choices & Consequences

The genre's soul is **choice and consequence**:
- **Branching decisions** — dialogue and quest choices that genuinely diverge the story, relationships, and outcomes. Choices should *matter* (see visual-novel-and-branching-narrative), not be cosmetic.
- **Hard dilemmas** — moral ambiguity, no clean "right answer," competing values — engaging players' judgment. The best choices haunt.
- **Consequences** — decisions ripple: NPCs die or help, factions turn, areas change, endings differ. Delayed consequences (a small early choice mattering much later) are especially powerful.

## Reactivity & World-State

What elevates CRPGs is **a world that remembers and reacts**:
- **Reactivity** — the game acknowledges your choices, build, and history: dialogue references your deeds, NPCs react to your reputation/appearance/past actions, quests resolve differently. This makes the world feel alive and *your playthrough* feel authored by you.
- **World-state tracking** — extensive flags/variables recording your choices, driving reactive content (see dialogue-and-narrative-systems). Deep reactivity is expensive but is the genre's signature value.
- **Player expression** — you should feel the story is *yours*, shaped by your decisions and character.

## Dialogue & Skill Checks

- **Rich dialogue trees** — conversation is core gameplay; talking your way through situations is often as important as combat. Writing quality carries the genre.
- **Skill checks** — using character stats/skills in dialogue and the world (persuade with high Charisma, pick a lock, spot a lie, hack a terminal). This ties your *build* to *narrative* options — a defining CRPG mechanic (Disco Elysium built an entire game on it).
- Checks create build-dependent paths and roleplay expression.

## Multiple Solutions

CRPGs prize **multiple valid approaches** (overlaps immersive-sim philosophy — see immersive-sim-and-systemic-design):
- A quest solvable by combat, stealth, diplomacy, bribery, or skills — respecting different builds and playstyles.
- The player's *creativity and character* determine the path. "There's more than one way" is a core value.

## Non-Linear Questing

- **Open or semi-open structure** — freedom in *what* to do and *in what order*; side quests with their own arcs and reactivity.
- **Quest design** with branches, moral complexity, and consequences — side content that's meaningful, not filler.
- Balancing freedom with coherent pacing and a satisfying main arc is the structural challenge.

Design CRPGs around **player-authored characters and roleplay**, **meaningful branching choices with real consequences**, deep **reactivity and world-state** that make the world respond to you, **dialogue with build-based skill checks**, **multiple solutions** honoring different playstyles, and **non-linear questing** — creating a reactive, personal story that the player genuinely authors through who they are and what they choose. The magic: *your* playthrough is uniquely yours.
