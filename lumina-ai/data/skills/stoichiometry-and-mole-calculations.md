# Stoichiometry & Mole Calculations

Stoichiometry is the quantitative accounting of chemistry — using balanced equations to calculate *how much* reactant is needed or product is formed. It's essential to chemistry exams at every level and the practical core of the subject. Everything hinges on the **mole**.

## The Mole Concept

- A **mole** is a counting unit: **6.022×10²³** (Avogadro's number) of particles — atoms, molecules, ions. It bridges the atomic scale and grams we can weigh.
- **Molar mass (M)** — mass of one mole (g/mol), numerically the atomic/molecular mass (from the periodic table). Water = 18 g/mol.
- **Key conversions**: **moles = mass/molar mass** (n = m/M); number of particles = n × Nₐ. The mole is the hub connecting mass, particles, and (for gases) volume.

## Balancing Equations

- A **balanced equation** has equal atoms of each element on both sides (conservation of mass — see chemical-bonding-and-reactions). Adjust **coefficients** (never subscripts).
- The coefficients give the **mole ratios** — the heart of every stoichiometry calculation.

## Mole Ratios (The Core)

- From the balanced equation, coefficients give ratios: in 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, 2 mol H₂ react with 1 mol O₂ to make 2 mol H₂O.
- **The universal method**: convert given amount → **moles** → use the **mole ratio** to find moles of what you want → convert back to mass/volume/particles. "Grams → moles → ratio → moles → grams."

## Limiting Reagent

- When reactants aren't in exact stoichiometric proportion, one runs out first — the **limiting reagent** — determining how much product forms. The other is in **excess**.
- **Method** — compute moles of product each reactant *could* make; the smallest wins (that reactant is limiting). A crucial, commonly-tested skill.

## Percent Yield

- **Theoretical yield** — the maximum product from stoichiometry. **Actual yield** — what's really obtained (less, due to side reactions, losses).
- **Percent yield = (actual/theoretical) × 100%** — measures reaction efficiency. Real chemistry rarely hits 100%.

## Concentration & Gas Calculations

- **Molarity** — concentration = moles of solute / liters of solution (mol/L). Used for solution stoichiometry (titrations — see acid-base-and-ph-equilibria).
- **Gases** — at STP, 1 mole of any ideal gas ≈ 22.4 L; more generally use **PV = nRT** (see kinetic-theory-and-gas-laws) to link moles, pressure, volume, temperature.
- **Percent composition** and **empirical/molecular formulas** — derive formulas from mass data.

Master stoichiometry via the **mole concept** (Avogadro's number, molar mass, n = m/M as the central bridge), **balanced equations** giving **mole ratios**, the universal **grams→moles→ratio→moles→grams** method, **limiting reagents** (which reactant runs out and caps the product), **percent yield** (efficiency), and **concentration/gas calculations** (molarity, PV = nRT). Mastering mole-based reasoning is the quantitative foundation of all chemistry — the skill that turns balanced equations into real amounts.
