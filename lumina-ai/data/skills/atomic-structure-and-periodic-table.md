# Atomic Structure & the Periodic Table

Everything is made of **atoms**, and their structure — especially their electrons — explains all of chemistry. The periodic table isn't an arbitrary chart; it's a profound organization of the elements that *predicts* their behavior from atomic structure. Understanding atoms and the table is the foundation of chemistry.

## Atomic Structure

An atom has:
- **Nucleus** — a dense core of **protons** (positive charge) and **neutrons** (neutral), holding almost all the mass.
- **Electrons** — tiny negative particles occupying the space around the nucleus (in "shells"/orbitals), balancing the protons' charge in a neutral atom.

Key numbers:
- **Atomic number (Z)** — number of protons; *defines the element* (6 protons = carbon, always). This is what the periodic table is ordered by.
- **Mass number** — protons + neutrons.
- **Isotopes** — same element, different neutron counts (same chemistry, different mass — e.g. carbon-12 vs carbon-14).
- **Ions** — atoms that gained/lost electrons, becoming charged.

## Electron Shells & Configuration

Electrons occupy **shells** (energy levels) around the nucleus, filling from lowest energy up:
- Each shell holds a limited number (2, 8, 8, 18…); electrons fill orbitals (s, p, d, f) in order.
- **Valence electrons** — those in the outermost shell — determine an element's **chemical behavior** (how it bonds — see chemical-bonding). This is the crucial insight.
- Atoms are most stable with a **full outer shell** (the "octet rule" — 8 valence electrons, like the noble gases). This drive to fill the outer shell explains *why* atoms react and bond.

## Organization of the Periodic Table

The table is arranged so structure predicts behavior:
- **Periods (rows)** — each row fills a new electron shell; going across, atomic number increases one at a time.
- **Groups (columns)** — elements in the same column have the **same number of valence electrons**, so they behave *chemically similarly*. Group 1 (alkali metals) all have 1 valence electron and are violently reactive; Group 18 (noble gases) have full shells and are inert.
- **Blocks** (s, p, d, f) correspond to which orbital is filling; metals (left/center), nonmetals (upper right), metalloids between.

The genius: position on the table tells you an element's electron structure, and thus its properties.

## Periodic Trends

Properties change *predictably* across and down the table:
- **Atomic radius** — decreases across a period (stronger nuclear pull), increases down a group (more shells).
- **Electronegativity** — the pull on shared electrons; increases across (toward fluorine), decreases down. Governs bond types.
- **Ionization energy** — energy to remove an electron; increases across, decreases down.
- **Reactivity** — metals most reactive lower-left, nonmetals most reactive upper-right; noble gases nearly inert.

These trends let you *predict* behavior from an element's position without memorizing each one.

## Why Elements Behave as They Do

The unifying idea: **an element's chemistry is determined by its valence electrons and drive toward a full outer shell.** Metals (few valence electrons) tend to *lose* them (form positive ions); nonmetals (nearly full shells) tend to *gain* them (negative ions) or share them. This single principle, encoded in the table's structure, explains bonding, reactivity, and the patterns of the entire chemical world.

Reason about atoms and elements via **atomic structure** (protons define the element, electrons drive chemistry), **electron shells and valence electrons** (outer shell determines behavior; octet stability), the **periodic table's organization** (periods fill shells, groups share valence counts and behavior), and **periodic trends** (radius, electronegativity, reactivity predictable from position). The periodic table is a map of atomic structure that predicts how all matter behaves.
