# Hero Shooter Design

Hero shooters (Overwatch, Valorant, Apex Legends, Paladins) blend FPS gunplay with **distinct heroes who each have unique abilities and roles** — fusing shooter mechanics with MOBA-style character diversity and team strategy. The player picks a hero, and the game becomes about ability usage, team composition, and coordination, not just aim. It's the shooter genre's most team-strategic form.

## The Hero Roster

The defining feature is a **cast of unique characters**:
- Each hero has a **distinct kit** — a signature weapon, several abilities, and an **ultimate** — creating wholly different playstyles within the same game (a shielded tank plays nothing like a flanking assassin or a support healer).
- **Roles/archetypes** — tank (frontline/space), damage (kills), support/healer (sustain/utility), and subclasses. Roles are interdependent — a team needs a mix.
- Kits are the design centerpiece: memorable, mechanically interesting, counterable, and distinct. Ability design > raw gunplay tuning here (though gunplay still matters — see fps-and-shooter-design).

## Team Composition & Synergy

Hero shooters are deeply **team-based**:
- **Composition** — the mix of heroes/roles a team fields dramatically affects effectiveness. Balanced comps (tank + damage + support) vs gimmick comps; adapting your pick to the team is core strategy.
- **Synergy** — abilities that combine (a barrier enabling a push, a stun setting up a teamfight ultimate, a healer sustaining a dive). Coordinated ability combos win fights — the MOBA-like teamplay layer.
- **Role queue / composition rules** often enforce balanced teams to prevent degenerate all-one-role comps.

## Ultimate Economy

A signature mechanic: **ultimates** — powerful abilities charged over time/actions:
- Ults are game-swinging; **tracking and timing** them (yours and the enemy's), and **coordinating** ults for combos, is high-level play. "Ultimate economy" — who has ults, spending vs saving them — is a strategic layer atop the shooting.
- Balancing ult charge rate and power is critical: too frequent = ultimate spam; too rare = irrelevant.

## Counter-Picking

Because heroes are asymmetric, **counterplay through hero selection** is central:
- Some heroes hard-counter others (anti-dive, anti-air, shield-break). **Swapping heroes** mid-match to counter the enemy comp is a key strategic decision (in games that allow it).
- This creates a metagame of picks and counters (see moba-design's counter web) layered over mechanical play. Reading the enemy and adapting your pick is a skill of its own.

## Objective-Based Modes

Hero shooters usually center on **objectives**, not just deathmatch:
- Payload escort, point capture, control, bomb defuse (Valorant) — objectives force engagements, teamwork, and role usage, and give matches structure and comeback potential.
- Objective design shapes how comps and abilities are valued (some heroes shine on attack vs defense).

## Balancing Asymmetric Heroes

The genre's brutal challenge (shared with MOBAs): **balancing dozens of unique characters**:
- **No unbeatable hero or comp** — every kit needs counterplay. Buff the weak, nerf the oppressive, chasing *diversity* (many viable picks) across skill tiers.
- **Ability + gunplay interaction** — balance both the shooting and the abilities, and their combinations.
- **Constant patching** and data-driven tuning (pick/win rates); the meta is meant to shift. New heroes must fit without breaking balance.
- **Accessibility vs depth** — heroes span skill floors/ceilings so different players find a fit.

Design hero shooters around a **roster of distinct, counterable hero kits** in **interdependent roles**, deep **team composition and ability synergy**, an **ultimate economy** as a strategic layer, **counter-picking** metagame, and **objective-based modes** — all held together by relentless **balance of asymmetric characters** for diversity and counterplay. The genre fuses shooter skill with team strategy: aim matters, but *who you pick, how you combo, and how you coordinate* matter more.
