# Trigonometry & Functions (THPT Math)

Trigonometry studies the relationships between angles and lengths, extended into **periodic functions** that model waves, rotations, and cycles. Together with the general concept of a **function**, it's a core pillar of high-school math (Toán THPT) and the foundation for calculus and physics.

## Trigonometric Ratios

In a right triangle, for an angle θ:
- **sin θ** = opposite/hypotenuse, **cos θ** = adjacent/hypotenuse, **tan θ** = opposite/adjacent = sin/cos.
- Reciprocals: **cot, sec, csc**.
- **SOH-CAH-TOA** — the mnemonic. These ratios let you find unknown sides/angles in triangles (with the **law of sines** and **law of cosines** for non-right triangles).

## The Unit Circle & Radians

Trig generalizes beyond triangles via the **unit circle** (radius 1):
- Any angle corresponds to a point (cos θ, sin θ) on the circle. This extends sin/cos to *all* angles (including >90°, negative), giving them their periodic nature.
- **Radians** — the natural angle unit (2π rad = 360°); an angle in radians = arc length on the unit circle. Calculus and physics use radians. Key angles (0, π/6, π/4, π/3, π/2…) and their exact values are worth memorizing.
- **Signs by quadrant** (ASTC — "All Students Take Calculus") tell you where each function is positive.

## Identities

Trig **identities** are equations true for all angles — essential for simplifying and solving:
- **Pythagorean**: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 (the master identity, from the unit circle).
- **Angle sum/difference**: sin(a±b), cos(a±b), tan(a±b).
- **Double-angle, half-angle**, product-to-sum, and sum-to-product formulas.
These let you transform expressions and are heavily tested in THPT.

## Graphs & Periodicity

- **y = sin x** and **y = cos x** — smooth waves oscillating between −1 and 1, period 2π. **y = tan x** — period π, with vertical asymptotes.
- **Transformations**: y = A·sin(Bx + C) + D — **A** = amplitude, **B** sets period (2π/B), **C** = phase shift, **D** = vertical shift. This models real periodic phenomena (sound, light, tides, AC current — see oscillations-and-waves).
- Periodicity — the repeating nature — is what makes trig functions model cycles.

## Solving Trig Equations

- Solving equations like sin x = ½ or 2cos²x − 1 = 0 uses identities and the unit circle to find **all** solutions (infinitely many, differing by the period — express with +k·2π or +kπ, k ∈ ℤ).
- A staple THPT skill: reduce to a basic form, find the reference solutions, add the period. Watch domain restrictions.

## Functions (General Concept)

Trig functions are instances of the broader idea of a **function** — a rule mapping each input to exactly one output:
- **Domain** (valid inputs) and **range** (possible outputs). tan has a restricted domain (undefined where cos = 0).
- **Composition** — f(g(x)), applying one function to another's output.
- **Inverse functions** — undo a function (arcsin, arccos, arctan invert the trig functions on restricted domains); a function has an inverse if it's one-to-one.
- **Even/odd, increasing/decreasing, periodicity** — properties used to analyze behavior (leading into derivatives — see calculus-intuition).

Master THPT trigonometry via the **ratios** (SOH-CAH-TOA, laws of sines/cosines), the **unit circle and radians** (extending trig to all angles, periodicity), **identities** (Pythagorean, angle-sum, double-angle) for simplifying, **graphs** (amplitude/period/phase), **solving trig equations** (all solutions with the period), and the general **function** concepts (domain, composition, inverse). These power the exam's trig and function problems and underpin calculus and wave physics.
