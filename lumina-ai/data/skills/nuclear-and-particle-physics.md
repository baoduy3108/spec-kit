# Nuclear & Particle Physics (Advanced Physics)

Nuclear physics studies the atom's core — the nucleus — where enormous energies are stored and released. It's an advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) topic (vật lý hạt nhân) combining Einstein's mass-energy equivalence with radioactivity and the reactions that power stars and reactors.

## The Nucleus

- The nucleus contains **protons** (Z, defining the element) and **neutrons** (N), together **nucleons** (A = Z + N, the mass number). Notation: ᴬ_Z X.
- **Isotopes** — same Z, different N (same element, different mass).
- The **strong nuclear force** binds nucleons against proton-proton repulsion (very short range, very strong).

## Mass-Energy Equivalence & Binding Energy

- **E = mc²** — mass and energy are equivalent; a tiny mass converts to enormous energy (c² is huge). The foundation of nuclear energy.
- **Mass defect** — a nucleus weighs *less* than its separate nucleons; the missing mass (Δm) became **binding energy** (E = Δmc²) holding it together.
- **Binding energy per nucleon** — peaks around iron (Fe-56). Nuclei lighter than iron release energy by **fusion**; heavier ones by **fission** — this curve explains where nuclear energy comes from.

## Radioactivity

Unstable nuclei **decay**, emitting radiation:
- **Alpha (α)** — emits a helium nucleus (⁴₂He); Z decreases by 2, A by 4. Heavy, low penetration.
- **Beta (β)** — a neutron converts to a proton + electron (β⁻, Z increases by 1) or vice versa (β⁺); emits an electron/positron. Medium penetration.
- **Gamma (γ)** — high-energy photon (EM wave) released as the nucleus de-excites; no change in Z or A. Highly penetrating.
- **Decay equations** must conserve nucleon number (A) and charge (Z).

## Half-Life & Decay Law

- **Half-life (T)** — the time for half a sample to decay; a fixed property of each isotope.
- **Decay law** — **N = N₀·(½)^(t/T) = N₀·e^(−λt)**, exponential decay (see exponentials-and-logarithms), where λ = ln2/T is the decay constant. **Activity** (decays per second) = λN.
- Used in radiometric **dating** (carbon-14), medicine, and a common calculation type.

## Fission & Fusion

- **Fission** — a heavy nucleus (uranium-235) splits into lighter ones + neutrons, releasing energy. A **chain reaction** (released neutrons trigger more fissions) powers reactors and bombs.
- **Fusion** — light nuclei (hydrogen isotopes) combine into heavier ones (helium), releasing even more energy per mass. Powers the **Sun and stars**; the goal of fusion energy research.
- Both release energy because products have higher binding energy per nucleon (a small mass converts to energy via E = mc²).

## Elementary Particles

- Beyond protons/neutrons: the **Standard Model** — **quarks** (make up protons/neutrons), **leptons** (electrons, neutrinos), and **force carriers** (photon, gluon, W/Z bosons, Higgs).
- Four fundamental forces: strong, electromagnetic, weak, gravity. A conceptual capstone (usually light in exams but foundational).

Master nuclear physics via the **nucleus** (protons/neutrons, isotopes, strong force), **mass-energy equivalence and binding energy** (E = mc², mass defect, the binding-energy-per-nucleon curve), **radioactivity** (α/β/γ decay with A/Z conservation), the **half-life decay law** (N = N₀(½)^(t/T), exponential), and **fission/fusion** (energy from binding-energy differences, chain reactions, stellar fusion). The unifying idea — tiny mass differences convert to vast energy via E = mc² — is the heart of gifted-exam nuclear physics.
