# Survival Horror Design

Survival horror makes the player feel **vulnerable, tense, and afraid** — not powerful. Where action games empower, horror *disempowers*: scarce resources, dangerous enemies, and an oppressive atmosphere keep the player anxious. The craft is engineering sustained dread and the exquisite relief that follows it.

## Vulnerability & Scarcity

The foundation is **making the player feel weak and under-resourced**:
- **Resource scarcity** — ammo, healing, and saves are rationed. Every bullet and herb is a decision; running low is terrifying. Scarcity *creates* tension in every encounter ("do I fight or run? can I afford to?").
- **Limited combat** — the player is *not* a super-soldier. Weapons are weak/clumsy, aiming deliberate, enemies tanky and threatening. Combat is a last resort with real cost, not a power fantasy. **Avoidance** (sneaking, running) is often the smart play.
- Feeling capable of anything kills horror; feeling barely-able-to-cope sustains it.

## Tension Over Jump Scares

The genre's real fear is **dread** — sustained anticipation — not cheap startles:
- **Jump scares** are a spice (a sudden shock), not the meal. Overused, they become predictable and lose power; players brace and the fear fades.
- **Dread** is the slow build — the sense something is *about to* happen, the sound down the hall, the door you must open. Anticipation of horror is scarier than horror itself; the player's imagination does the work.
- **The unseen** — what you *can't* see is more frightening than what you can. Darkness, obscured threats, off-screen sounds. Reveal too much and the monster becomes mundane.

## Atmosphere

Horror is built from **audiovisual craft**:
- **Lighting** — darkness, limited light sources (flashlight with dying batteries), shadows that hide threats. Light management is often a mechanic *and* a fear generator.
- **Sound design** — arguably the most important tool: ambient dread, sudden stings, distant noises, silence (which is terrifying), the enemy you *hear* before you see. Audio cues manipulate anticipation directly.
- **Environment** — unsettling, oppressive spaces; environmental storytelling implying past horrors (see open-world environmental storytelling). A believable, decaying place.

## Enemies

- **Empowered, threatening enemies** — dangerous, sometimes unkillable (a stalker that hunts you relentlessly), forcing flight and panic.
- **Uncertainty** — not knowing where the threat is, or if it's coming, keeps dread high. Relentless/unpredictable pursuers (the "nemesis" type) are a genre staple.
- Restraint — fewer, scarier enemies beat hordes; overexposure breeds familiarity, and familiarity kills fear.

## The Fear-Relief Rhythm

Horror can't be 100% intensity — that exhausts and numbs the player. Great pacing is a **rhythm of tension and release**:
- **Build dread → payoff (or a fake-out) → brief relief → build again.** Quiet "safe" stretches make the scares land harder by contrast; unrelenting terror becomes background noise.
- **Safe rooms** — designated calm spaces (often with a save point and soothing music) offer respite and let the player breathe, plan, and manage inventory. The *contrast* between the safe room and the hostile world sharpens both.

## Save & Inventory Tension

- **Limited/tension-laden saving** (scarce save resources, or saves only in safe rooms) makes progress precious and death costly — heightening every risk. (Modern games often relax this for accessibility — a real trade-off with tension.)
- **Inventory management** — limited space forces hard choices (carry ammo or healing or a key item?), adding a strategic layer and a feeling of scarcity even in menus.

Design survival horror by **disempowering the player** through scarcity and limited combat, generating **dread over jump scares** via the unseen and anticipation, crafting **atmosphere through lighting and (especially) sound**, deploying **scarce but threatening enemies**, and pacing a **fear-relief rhythm** with safe-room respites — so the player feels vulnerable, tense, and truly afraid, and every survival is relief earned.
