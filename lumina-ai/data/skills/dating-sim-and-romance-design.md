# Dating Sim & Romance Design

Dating sims and romance mechanics (Stardew Valley's marriages, Persona's social links, otome games, Mass Effect romances) let players **build relationships with characters**, usually toward friendship or romance. The design challenge is making relationship progression feel *earned and emotionally believable* — not a transactional "insert gifts, receive love" vending machine. Good romance design is as much writing as systems.

## Relationship/Affection Meters

The mechanical backbone is a **tracked relationship level** per character:
- **Affection/friendship points** (often shown as hearts/levels) that rise through positive interactions and fall through neglect or wrong choices.
- **Level gates** — reaching thresholds unlocks new dialogue, events, story beats, and eventually romance/confession. This paces the relationship's deepening.
- The risk: making love feel like a *number to grind*. The systems should *support* an emotional arc, not replace it — the meter is scaffolding for a story, not the point.

## Romanceable Characters with Distinct Arcs

- **Distinct love interests** — each with a unique personality, backstory, likes/dislikes, and **character arc** that unfolds as the relationship deepens. Variety lets players find someone who resonates.
- **Progression as revelation** — deepening the relationship reveals the character's depths, vulnerabilities, and growth (their "route"). The best romances are about *getting to know someone*, with meaningful story, not just rising numbers.
- **Reactivity** — characters remember your choices and respond, making the bond feel personal (see crpg reactivity).

## Interaction Systems

How players build relationships:
- **Gifts** — giving items a character likes raises affection (know their tastes); a classic, but shouldn't be the *only* path or it feels transactional.
- **Dialogue choices** — saying the right things, expressing interest, making choices that align with the character. Conversation is the emotional core.
- **Spending time / shared activities** — dates, events, helping with their problems. Presence and attention as the currency of connection.
- **Stat/skill requirements** — some games gate romance content behind player stats (Persona's social stats), tying self-improvement to relationships.

## Time Management

Many dating sims layer a **time-management/resource core**:
- Limited time (days, energy, actions) forces **choosing whom to pursue** and how to split attention (see farming-and-life-sim-design). You can't romance everyone at once — meaningful trade-offs.
- This scarcity makes relationship-building a *strategic* as well as emotional activity, and gives replay value (different routes each playthrough).

## Romance Routes & Gating

- **Routes** — dedicated story paths per love interest (strong in visual-novel-style dating sims — see visual-novel-and-branching-narrative). Committing to a route may lock out others, giving weight and replayability.
- **Confession/commitment beats** — climactic moments (asking out, confession, marriage) as emotional payoffs earned through the relationship.
- **Multiple endings** per character based on your choices and affection.

## Writing Believable Emotional Progression

The genre lives or dies on **writing**:
- **Earned intimacy** — the relationship should *develop* through meaningful interactions and revelations, so affection feels justified, not automatic. Rushed or purely transactional romance rings hollow.
- **Character depth and chemistry** — believable personalities, genuine emotional beats, and a sense that this person is *someone*, not a prize.
- **Respect and agency** — healthy portrayals; the player and characters have agency (avoid creepy/coercive framings). Consent and mutual interest matter.

Design dating sims/romance around **relationship meters that scaffold (not replace) an emotional arc**, **distinct romanceable characters with real depth and reactivity**, varied **interaction systems** (dialogue, gifts, shared time) beyond pure gift-grinding, **time-management trade-offs** that make attention meaningful, and **routes with earned emotional payoffs** — all carried by **believable writing** that makes affection feel *earned*. The magic isn't the number going up; it's the story of two characters genuinely connecting.
