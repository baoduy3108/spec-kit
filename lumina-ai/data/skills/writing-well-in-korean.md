# Writing Well in Korean

Korean, like Japanese, weaves **social hierarchy and politeness into its grammar** through speech levels and honorifics — choosing the right level of politeness for the relationship is central and unavoidable. It's written in **Hangul**, a uniquely systematic alphabet, and follows SOV order with particles marking grammatical roles.

## Hangul: The Alphabet

Korean uses **Hangul (한글)** — a featural alphabet where letters combine into **syllable blocks** (한 = ㅎ+ㅏ+ㄴ). Designed for the language, it's phonetic, logical, and learnable quickly. Modern Korean is written almost entirely in Hangul (Chinese characters, **Hanja 한자**, appear rarely now — occasionally for disambiguation or in formal/academic contexts). Writing well means clean, correct Hangul with proper spacing (word spacing matters in Korean) and spelling.

## Speech Levels & Honorifics (The Core Skill)

Korean grammatically encodes politeness through **speech levels** (verb endings) and **honorifics**:
- **Banmal (반말)** — casual/intimate speech; close friends, younger people, family. Using it with the wrong person is very rude.
- **Jondaenmal (존댓말)** — polite/respectful speech; the safe default with strangers, elders, superiors. Levels within it range from polite (-요) to formal (-습니다, for formal/business/broadcast).
- **Honorifics** — the honorific infix -(으)시- and special honorific vocabulary elevate the person being discussed (their actions, even their existence: 계시다 vs 있다). Humble forms lower yourself.

Choosing the right level based on **age, status, and closeness** is essential and constant — Korean society is hierarchy-conscious, and language reflects it in every sentence. Getting it wrong (banmal to an elder) is a serious social error. This is the heart of appropriate Korean.

## Particles & SOV Order

- **Subject-Object-Verb** — the verb comes last, like Japanese.
- **Particles** mark roles: 은/는 (topic), 이/가 (subject), 을/를 (object), 에 (location/time), 에서 (from/at), 에게/한테 (to a person), 로/으로 (by/toward), 의 (possessive), 도 (also), 만 (only). Particles have forms depending on whether the preceding word ends in a consonant or vowel (은 vs 는). Correct particle use is fundamental.

## Topic vs Subject Markers

Like Japanese wa/ga, Korean distinguishes **은/는 (topic — "as for…")** from **이/가 (subject — often new/emphasized info)**. The nuance shifts emphasis and is a subtle mark of fluency. Getting it right makes prose flow naturally.

## Formality & Natural Phrasing

- **Register** — from intimate banmal to formal 합니다-style (news, business, official writing). Written Korean has formal conventions; casual writing (texting, social media) uses banmal, slang, and emoticons.
- **Implicitness** — subjects and pronouns are **frequently dropped** when clear from context; over-using "I/you" (저/제/너/당신 — and note 당신 for "you" is actually awkward/rare) sounds unnatural. Korean prefers implication and titles/roles over pronouns (calling someone by their title/name+honorific rather than "you").
- **Indirectness** — politeness favors softened, indirect phrasing.

Write Korean well by using clean **Hangul** with proper spacing, above all choosing the correct **speech level and honorifics** for the social relationship (age/status/closeness — the central skill), using **particles** correctly in **SOV** order (with consonant/vowel forms), handling the **topic/subject (은는/이가)** nuance, and embracing Korean **implicitness** (drop clear subjects, avoid awkward "you," use titles). The soul of good Korean is respectful, hierarchy-aware, appropriately-polite expression for exactly whom you're addressing.
