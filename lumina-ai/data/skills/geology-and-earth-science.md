# Geology & Earth Science

Geology studies the **solid Earth** — its materials, structure, and the processes shaping it over vast time. It reveals a dynamic planet: continents drifting, mountains rising, rocks recycling. It builds on physical geography (see physical-geography-and-climate) with a focus on rocks, deep time, and Earth's interior.

## Earth's Structure

- **Layers** — thin solid **crust**, hot slowly-flowing **mantle**, liquid **outer core**, and solid iron **inner core**. Heat from the core (and radioactive decay) drives geological activity.
- **The magnetic field** — generated by the churning liquid outer core, shielding Earth from solar radiation.

## Plate Tectonics

The unifying theory of geology:
- The crust + upper mantle (lithosphere) is broken into **plates** that move slowly on the flowing mantle beneath, driven by convection.
- **Boundaries** — **convergent** (collide → mountains, volcanoes, trenches, earthquakes), **divergent** (spread → mid-ocean ridges, rifts), **transform** (slide → earthquakes).
- Explains continental drift, the distribution of earthquakes/volcanoes, mountain building, and the shapes of continents (once joined as Pangaea).

## Rocks & the Rock Cycle

Three rock types, continually transformed:
- **Igneous** — cooled from molten magma/lava (granite, basalt).
- **Sedimentary** — compacted sediments/layers (sandstone, limestone; often contain fossils).
- **Metamorphic** — altered by heat and pressure (marble from limestone, slate from shale).
- **The rock cycle** — rocks slowly convert between types (melting, weathering, sedimentation, metamorphism) over geological time.
- **Minerals** — the crystalline building blocks of rocks (quartz, feldspar), identified by properties (hardness, cleavage, color).

## Geological Time & Fossils

- **Deep time** — Earth is ~4.6 billion years old; geological processes act over unimaginable spans. The **geologic time scale** (eons, eras, periods) organizes this history.
- **Fossils** — preserved remains/traces in sedimentary rock; record past life and evolution (see evolution), and let geologists date and correlate rock layers (**relative dating** by strata, **absolute dating** by radioactive decay — see nuclear physics).
- **Principles** — superposition (lower layers older), uniformitarianism (present processes explain the past).

## Earthquakes & Volcanoes

- **Earthquakes** — sudden release of stress along faults (plate boundaries), sending **seismic waves**; measured by magnitude (Richter/moment scales — logarithmic).
- **Volcanoes** — molten rock (magma) erupting at the surface, mostly at plate boundaries and hotspots. Both are hazards and clues to Earth's interior.

## Surface Processes

- **Weathering** (breaking down rock — physical and chemical) and **erosion** (transport by water, wind, ice, gravity) sculpt landscapes; **deposition** builds new features (deltas, dunes).
- **The water cycle, glaciers, rivers** continuously reshape the surface (see physical-geography).

Understand geology via **Earth's layered structure** (crust to core, heat-driven), **plate tectonics** (moving plates building mountains, volcanoes, and earthquakes — the unifying theory), the **rock cycle** (igneous/sedimentary/metamorphic rocks and minerals transforming over time), **geological time and fossils** (deep time, dating strata, the record of life), **earthquakes and volcanoes**, and **surface processes** (weathering, erosion, deposition). Geology reveals Earth as an ancient, dynamic, constantly-recycling planet — the physical stage for all of life and geography.
