# Molecular Biology & Gene Expression

Molecular biology explains how the instructions in DNA become the working machinery of life. At its core is a remarkably universal information flow shared by nearly all organisms — understanding it underpins genetics, biotech, medicine, and bioinformatics.

## DNA: The Information Store

**DNA** (deoxyribonucleic acid) stores genetic information as a sequence of four **bases** — A, T, G, C — along a sugar-phosphate backbone, twisted into the famous **double helix**. The two strands are **complementary** (A pairs with T, G with C), so each strand templates the other — the basis of faithful copying. **Replication** duplicates the entire genome before cell division, each strand serving as a template for a new partner (semi-conservative).

A **gene** is a stretch of DNA encoding a functional product (usually a protein). The full set is the **genome**.

## The Central Dogma

The universal flow of genetic information: **DNA → RNA → protein.**
1. **Transcription** — a gene's DNA is copied into **messenger RNA (mRNA)** by RNA polymerase. (In eukaryotes, introns are spliced out, exons joined.)
2. **Translation** — ribosomes read the mRNA and build a **protein** — a chain of amino acids — which folds into a 3D shape that does the work (enzymes, structure, signaling; see how proteins fold).

Proteins are the "doers" of the cell; DNA is the blueprint; RNA is the messenger between.

## The Genetic Code

mRNA is read in **codons** — triplets of bases, each specifying one amino acid (or a stop signal). With 4 bases, there are 64 codons for 20 amino acids, so the code is **redundant** (several codons per amino acid) — this buffers some mutations. The code is **nearly universal** across life, which is why genes can be transferred between species (the foundation of genetic engineering).

## Gene Regulation

Every cell has the *same* DNA, yet a neuron differs from a muscle cell — because **which genes are expressed, when, and how much** differs. **Gene regulation** controls expression:
- **Transcription factors** bind DNA to turn genes on/off.
- **Promoters/enhancers** — regulatory regions controlling transcription.
- **Epigenetics** — chemical marks (methylation, histone modification) that alter expression *without* changing the DNA sequence, and can be heritable.
- Feedback loops, RNA regulation, and signaling integrate to tune expression dynamically.

Regulation is why one genome yields many cell types and responds to the environment — arguably where the real complexity of life lives.

## Mutations & Variation

Changes in DNA sequence — **mutations** (substitutions, insertions, deletions) — arise from copying errors, radiation, chemicals. Effects range from silent (redundant code) to changing a protein (missense) to breaking it (nonsense/frameshift). Mutations are the raw material of **evolution** (variation for selection to act on) and the cause of many diseases and cancers. Population-level variation (alleles, SNPs) underlies genetics and heredity.

## Genotype → Phenotype

The **genotype** (genetic makeup) produces the **phenotype** (observable traits) — but not simply. Gene expression, regulation, gene-gene interactions, and environment all mediate the path from sequence to trait. Most traits are **polygenic** (many genes) and environment-influenced, which is why "a gene for X" is usually an oversimplification.

## Why It Matters

This framework enables modern biotech: reading genomes (sequencing), editing them (CRISPR — see genome editing), engineering organisms, diagnosing genetic disease, and designing drugs. The central dogma is the operating system of biology, and molecular biology is how we read and increasingly write it.
