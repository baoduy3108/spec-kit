# Astronomy & Cosmology

Astronomy studies celestial objects; cosmology studies the **universe as a whole** — its origin, structure, and fate. From planets to the Big Bang, it's humanity's attempt to understand our place in a vast cosmos, built on physics (gravity, light, nuclear reactions — see gravitation-and-kepler, nuclear-and-particle-physics).

## The Solar System

- **The Sun** — a star at the center; a giant ball of plasma fusing hydrogen into helium (see nuclear physics), providing light and heat.
- **Planets** — orbit the Sun (Kepler's laws): rocky inner planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and gas/ice giants (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune). Plus moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, and comets.
- **Formed** ~4.6 billion years ago from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust.

## Stars & Their Life Cycles

- **Stars** — form in nebulae when gravity collapses gas until fusion ignites; they shine by **nuclear fusion** (H → He, releasing energy that balances gravity).
- **Life cycle** — a star lives most of its life on the "main sequence," then, as fuel runs out, expands into a **red giant**. Its fate depends on **mass**:
  - Low/medium mass (like the Sun) → sheds outer layers → **white dwarf**.
  - High mass → **supernova** explosion → **neutron star** or **black hole**.
- **Nucleosynthesis** — stars forge the elements (up to iron in cores, heavier in supernovae). "We are made of star stuff" — the atoms in us came from dead stars.

## Galaxies & Large-Scale Structure

- **Galaxies** — vast collections of billions of stars, gas, and dark matter bound by gravity (our **Milky Way** is a spiral galaxy). Types: spiral, elliptical, irregular.
- **Scale** — galaxies cluster into groups, clusters, and superclusters, forming a cosmic web across billions of light-years. The universe is unimaginably vast (a **light-year** = distance light travels in a year).

## The Big Bang & Expanding Universe

- **The Big Bang** — the universe began ~13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense state and has been **expanding** ever since. Not an explosion *in* space, but the expansion *of* space itself.
- **Evidence** — the **cosmic microwave background** (leftover heat), the **redshift** of distant galaxies (Hubble's law — everything receding, faster the farther away), and the abundance of light elements.
- **Accelerating expansion** — the expansion is speeding up (discovered via distant supernovae).

## Dark Matter & Dark Energy

- **Dark matter** — invisible mass (detected by its gravity) that holds galaxies together; ~27% of the universe, its nature unknown.
- **Dark energy** — a mysterious force driving the accelerating expansion; ~68% of the universe.
- **Ordinary matter** (everything we see) is only ~5%. The universe is mostly unknown — a frontier.

## Observing the Cosmos

- We learn about the universe almost entirely through **light** (and other EM radiation — see electromagnetism): telescopes across the spectrum (optical, radio, X-ray, infrared — the James Webb telescope), spectroscopy (revealing composition, motion, temperature), and now gravitational waves and neutrinos.
- **Looking out is looking back in time** — light from distant objects left long ago, so we see the past.

Explore the universe via the **solar system** (Sun, planets, formed from a gas cloud), **stars** (fusion-powered, with mass-dependent life cycles forging the elements), **galaxies** (billions of stars in a vast cosmic web), the **Big Bang** (a 13.8-billion-year expansion of space, evidenced by the CMB and redshift), the mysterious **dark matter and dark energy** (95% of the cosmos), and how we **observe** it through light across the spectrum. Astronomy and cosmology connect physics to the grandest questions about the origin, scale, and fate of everything.
