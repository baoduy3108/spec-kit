# Microbiology & Viruses (Advanced Biology)

Microbiology studies **microorganisms** — bacteria, viruses, fungi, protists — the invisible life forms that are the most abundant on Earth, driving ecosystems, causing disease, and powering biotechnology. An advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) biology topic bridging cell biology, genetics, and ecology.

## Bacteria

- **Prokaryotic** cells (see cell-biology-and-heredity) — no nucleus; DNA in a single loop plus small **plasmids**; a cell wall (targeted by antibiotics).
- **Reproduction** — **binary fission** (splitting in two), extremely fast (doubling in minutes under good conditions → exponential growth).
- **Genetic exchange** — conjugation, transformation, transduction transfer genes (including antibiotic resistance) between bacteria — no need for reproduction.
- **Diversity** — different shapes (cocci, bacilli, spirilla), metabolisms (aerobic/anaerobic, autotroph/heterotroph); found everywhere.

## Viruses

- **Non-living** (or borderline) — not cells; just genetic material (DNA or RNA) in a protein coat (**capsid**), sometimes an envelope. Can't reproduce or metabolize alone.
- **Obligate parasites** — must **hijack a host cell** to replicate:
  - **Lytic cycle** — virus injects its genes, forces the cell to make new viruses, then bursts (lyses) the cell, releasing them.
  - **Lysogenic cycle** — viral genes integrate into the host genome, replicating quietly with the cell until triggered into the lytic cycle later.
- **RNA viruses** (flu, HIV, coronaviruses) mutate fast (error-prone replication — see mutations), driving new strains and complicating vaccines/immunity (see immunology).
- **Bacteriophages** infect bacteria; used in research and phage therapy.

## Microbial Roles

Microbes are ecologically and practically vital:
- **Decomposers** — bacteria and fungi recycle dead matter and nutrients (nitrogen cycle, carbon cycle — see ecology-and-ecosystems). Life depends on them.
- **Symbiosis** — gut bacteria aid digestion; nitrogen-fixing bacteria feed plants.
- **Disease** — pathogenic microbes cause infections (see immunology-and-disease).
- **Biotechnology** — bacteria/yeast produce insulin, antibiotics, enzymes, fermented foods (bread, yogurt, alcohol — see fermentation in respiration), and are workhorses of genetic engineering (see molecular-genetics).

## Microbial Growth & Control

- **Growth phases** — lag, exponential (log), stationary, death — as nutrients and conditions change.
- **Controlling microbes** — **antibiotics** (kill/inhibit bacteria, not viruses), **antiseptics/disinfectants**, sterilization (heat, radiation), and antivirals. **Antibiotic resistance** (evolving via selection and gene transfer) is a critical global threat from overuse.

Master microbiology via **bacteria** (prokaryotic structure, fast binary fission, gene transfer including resistance), **viruses** (non-living gene-and-capsid particles that hijack host cells via lytic/lysogenic cycles, with fast-mutating RNA viruses), **microbial roles** (decomposers recycling nutrients, symbionts, pathogens, biotech producers), and **microbial growth and control** (growth phases, antibiotics vs antivirals, resistance). Understanding the biology of microbes — abundant, fast-evolving, and central to ecosystems, disease, and biotechnology — is the microbiological depth gifted-exam biology rewards.
