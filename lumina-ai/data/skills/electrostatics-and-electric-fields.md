# Electrostatics & Electric Fields (Advanced Physics)

Electrostatics — the physics of stationary charges — is a foundational, quantitative advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) topic (tĩnh điện). It develops the crucial concepts of **field** and **potential** that recur throughout physics, from Coulomb's force law up through capacitors and Gauss's law.

## Coulomb's Law

- The force between two point charges: **F = k·q₁q₂/r²** (k ≈ 9×10⁹ N·m²/C²), along the line joining them — repulsive for like charges, attractive for opposite.
- An inverse-square law (like gravity — see gravitation-and-kepler). For multiple charges, forces add as **vectors** (superposition).

## The Electric Field

- **Electric field E** — the force per unit charge at a point: **E = F/q** (N/C). It exists in the space around charges; a test charge placed there feels F = qE.
- **Field of a point charge**: E = kQ/r², pointing away from positive, toward negative.
- **Field lines** — visualize E: start on positive, end on negative charges; density shows strength; never cross. The field concept (action through a field, not at a distance) is central to all of physics.
- **Superposition** — total field is the vector sum of contributions.

## Gauss's Law

- **Gauss's law** — the electric **flux** through any closed surface equals the enclosed charge over ε₀: **Φ = Q_enc/ε₀**.
- A powerful shortcut: for symmetric charge distributions (sphere, line, plane), it gives the field without integrating Coulomb's law. E.g. field of an infinite plane is uniform; field inside a conductor is zero.
- One of Maxwell's equations (see electromagnetism-fundamentals).

## Electric Potential & Energy

- **Electric potential energy** — work to assemble charges (U = kq₁q₂/r for two charges).
- **Electric potential (voltage) V** — potential energy per unit charge: V = U/q (volts); for a point charge V = kQ/r. A *scalar* (easier than the vector field).
- **Relation** — the field points "downhill" in potential; E = −dV/dr (field is the potential gradient). **Equipotential surfaces** are perpendicular to field lines.
- **Work** done moving a charge = qΔV, independent of path (conservative field).

## Capacitors & Capacitance

- A **capacitor** stores charge and energy — two conductors separated by an insulator (dielectric).
- **Capacitance C = Q/V** (farads) — charge stored per volt; for a parallel plate C = ε₀εᵣA/d (bigger area, smaller gap, higher dielectric → more capacitance).
- **Combinations** — parallel capacitances add; series combine reciprocally (opposite of resistors).
- **Energy stored** — **W = ½CV² = ½QV = Q²/2C**, held in the electric field between the plates.

Master electrostatics via **Coulomb's law** (inverse-square force, vector superposition), the **electric field** (force per charge, field lines, the field concept), **Gauss's law** (flux = enclosed charge/ε₀, a shortcut for symmetric cases), **electric potential and energy** (scalar V = kQ/r, work = qΔV, E as the potential gradient), and **capacitors** (C = Q/V, combinations, energy ½CV²). The field and potential concepts developed here are among the most important in all of physics — exactly the depth gifted-exam electrostatics demands.
