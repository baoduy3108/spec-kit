# Inorganic Chemistry & Periodicity (Advanced Chemistry)

Inorganic chemistry covers the elements and their compounds (excluding most carbon chemistry). At the advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) level (hóa vô cơ), it's about understanding **periodic trends** deeply and the characteristic chemistry of element groups — turning the periodic table from a chart into a predictive tool.

## Periodic Trends (Deep)

Building on atomic structure (see atomic-structure-and-periodic-table), the trends explain reactivity:
- **Atomic radius** — decreases across a period (rising nuclear charge pulls electrons in), increases down a group (more shells).
- **Ionization energy** — increases across, decreases down (harder to remove electrons from smaller, more-tightly-held atoms).
- **Electronegativity** — increases across (toward F), decreases down.
- **Metallic character** — decreases across (metals left, nonmetals right), increases down.
These trends *predict* how elements bond and react — the core skill.

## Group Chemistry

Elements in a group behave similarly (same valence electrons):
- **Group 1 (alkali metals)** — 1 valence electron, very reactive, reactivity *increases* down the group; react vigorously with water forming hydroxides and H₂; form +1 ions.
- **Group 2 (alkaline earth)** — +2 ions, less reactive than group 1.
- **Group 17 (halogens)** — 7 valence electrons, very reactive nonmetals, reactivity *decreases* down the group; form −1 ions; a more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one from solution.
- **Group 18 (noble gases)** — full shells, nearly inert.
- **Transition metals** (see transition-metals-and-coordination) — variable oxidation states, coloured.

## Oxides & Acid-Base Character

- **Metal oxides** are generally **basic** (react with acids); **nonmetal oxides** are generally **acidic** (react with bases, form acids in water — CO₂ → carbonic acid, SO₂ → sulfurous).
- **Amphoteric oxides** (Al₂O₃, ZnO) react with both acids and bases.
- Acid-base character of oxides shifts across a period (basic → amphoteric → acidic) — a periodic trend.

## The Reactivity Series

- Metals ranked by reactivity (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, ... Cu, Ag, Au). More reactive metals:
  - Displace less reactive ones from compounds/solutions (single displacement).
  - React more readily with water, acids, and oxygen.
- Governs extraction methods (reactive metals need electrolysis; unreactive ones found native), corrosion, and displacement reactions. A key predictive tool.

## Descriptive Inorganic Reactions

- **Acid + metal** → salt + hydrogen; **acid + base/carbonate** → salt + water (+ CO₂); **displacement** reactions; **precipitation** (insoluble salts form on mixing solutions — used in qualitative analysis).
- **Qualitative analysis** — identifying ions by characteristic reactions, colours, precipitates, and flame tests. A practical exam component.

Master inorganic chemistry via **deep periodic trends** (radius, ionization energy, electronegativity, metallic character — predicting reactivity), **group chemistry** (alkali metals and halogens' characteristic, trend-following behaviour), **oxide acid-base character** (metal basic, nonmetal acidic, amphoteric), the **reactivity series** (displacement, extraction, corrosion), and **descriptive reactions** (acid-metal/base, precipitation, qualitative analysis). Using periodicity to *predict* element behaviour, rather than memorizing each, is the depth gifted-exam inorganic chemistry rewards.
