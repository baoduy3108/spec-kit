# JRPG Design & Conventions

JRPGs (Japanese-style RPGs — Final Fantasy, Dragon Quest, Persona) are a tradition emphasizing **authored story, memorable characters, and structured turn-based combat**. Where Western RPGs (see crpg) often prioritize player choice and open worlds, JRPGs typically deliver a **crafted, linear narrative experience** with distinctive battle and progression systems. It's as much a set of conventions and a *feel* as a mechanical genre.

## Authored Story & Characters

The heart of a JRPG is its **crafted narrative**:
- **Linear, authored plot** — a fixed, deliberately-told story with dramatic arcs, twists, and emotional beats. The player experiences a *designed* journey rather than authoring their own.
- **Fixed party characters** — pre-defined heroes with personalities, backstories, and arcs (vs a created blank-slate avatar). Character relationships and development are central; players grow attached to the cast.
- **Emphasis on emotion, theme, and spectacle** — cutscenes, dramatic music, and set-pieces. The story is usually the main draw.

## Turn-Based Battle Systems

Combat is typically **turn-based**, with signature variants:
- **Classic turn-based** — parties alternate; select commands (attack, magic, item, skill) from menus.
- **ATB (Active Time Battle)** — turns governed by charging time gauges, adding light real-time pressure (Final Fantasy's innovation).
- **Systems depth** — elemental weaknesses, status effects, buffs/debuffs, resource management (MP), and combos. Encounters are tactical puzzles solved through party abilities.
- **Distinctive twists** — press-turn, junction, materia, Persona-fusion — each series has signature battle mechanics that define its identity.

## Party & Job/Class Systems

- **Party building** — assemble a team with complementary roles (tank, healer, mage, DPS); party composition and turn-order tactics matter.
- **Job/class systems** — customizable roles (Final Fantasy's job system, skill trees) let players shape characters, balancing authored identity with build flexibility.
- **Progression** — leveling, learning skills, equipment (see rpg-systems-and-progression). The power curve is steady and central.

## Encounters & Grinding

- **Random encounters** (classic — battles trigger invisibly while walking) or **visible/symbol encounters** (modern — see enemies and choose to engage). Visible encounters are now generally preferred (more player agency, less frustration).
- **Level grinding** — a convention (sometimes criticized): fighting to gain levels/resources to overcome tougher content. Pacing grind vs story (letting normal play keep you strong enough) is a key balance — too much grind stalls the narrative.

## World & Dungeon Structure

- **Overworld/world map**, **towns** (shops, inns, NPCs, story), and **dungeons** (exploration, puzzles, encounters, bosses) — the classic loop of explore → town → dungeon → boss → advance story.
- **Fairly linear progression** with gating (get the ship/airship to open new areas), guiding the player through the authored arc, often opening up more later.

## Spectacle & Presentation

JRPGs lean into **audiovisual spectacle** — memorable soundtracks, dramatic boss battles, summon/spell animations, and emotional cutscenes. Presentation and *feel* (the drama, the music swell at a story climax) are core to the genre's appeal and identity.

Design JRPGs around a **crafted, character-driven narrative**, a **deep turn-based (or ATB) battle system** with a signature twist, **party and job/class building**, a **town-dungeon-boss world structure** with (ideally visible) encounters and well-paced progression, and strong **audiovisual spectacle** — delivering an authored, emotional adventure where story, characters, and tactical battles combine into a memorable journey.
