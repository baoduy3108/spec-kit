# Solutions & Colligative Properties (Advanced Chemistry)

Solutions — homogeneous mixtures of a solute dissolved in a solvent — are central to chemistry, and their quantitative behaviour is an advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) topic. Especially interesting are **colligative properties**, which depend only on the *number* of dissolved particles, not their identity.

## Dissolving & Solubility

- **Dissolving** — solvent molecules surround and separate solute particles. "**Like dissolves like**": polar solvents (water) dissolve polar/ionic solutes; nonpolar solvents dissolve nonpolar solutes (see chemical-bonding's polarity).
- **Solubility** — the maximum solute that dissolves at a given temperature (saturated solution). Usually rises with temperature for solids; **gases dissolve less at higher temperature** (and more at higher pressure — Henry's law).
- **Saturated, unsaturated, supersaturated** solutions.

## Concentration Units

Precisely quantifying "how much solute":
- **Molarity (M)** — moles solute / liter of solution (mol/L). The most common; used in stoichiometry and titrations.
- **Molality (m)** — moles solute / kg of solvent (temperature-independent, used for colligative properties).
- **Mole fraction, mass percent, ppm** — other measures. Converting between them is a common skill.

## Solubility Product (Ksp)

For sparingly-soluble ionic salts in equilibrium with their ions:
- **Ksp** — the equilibrium constant for dissolving: for AgCl ⇌ Ag⁺ + Cl⁻, Ksp = [Ag⁺][Cl⁻]. Smaller Ksp = less soluble.
- **Predicting precipitation** — compare the **ion product Q** to Ksp: Q > Ksp → precipitate forms; Q < Ksp → stays dissolved.
- **Common-ion effect** — adding an ion already in the equilibrium *decreases* solubility (Le Chatelier). Used in selective precipitation and analysis.

## Colligative Properties (The Key Topic)

Properties that depend on the **number of solute particles**, not their type:
- **Boiling-point elevation** — dissolved solute *raises* the boiling point: ΔTb = i·Kb·m (i = van't Hoff factor = particles per formula unit; NaCl gives i ≈ 2).
- **Freezing-point depression** — solute *lowers* the freezing point: ΔTf = i·Kf·m (why salt melts ice, antifreeze works).
- **Vapor-pressure lowering** — solute reduces the solvent's vapor pressure (Raoult's law) — the underlying cause of the boiling/freezing shifts.
- **Osmotic pressure** — Π = iMRT — the pressure driving solvent across a semipermeable membrane from dilute to concentrated (crucial in biology — cells, see human-physiology).

The **van't Hoff factor (i)** matters: ionic solutes dissociate into multiple particles, multiplying the effect. Colligative properties let you *determine molar masses* from measured temperature/pressure changes.

Master solution chemistry via **dissolving and solubility** ("like dissolves like," temperature/pressure effects), **concentration units** (molarity for stoichiometry, molality for colligative), the **solubility product Ksp** (predicting precipitation via Q vs Ksp, common-ion effect), and **colligative properties** (boiling-point elevation, freezing-point depression, osmotic pressure — all scaling with the *number* of particles via the van't Hoff factor). The elegant idea — that some properties count particles regardless of identity — is the conceptual highlight of gifted-exam solution chemistry.
