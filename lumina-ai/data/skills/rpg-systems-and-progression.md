# RPG Systems & Progression

Role-playing games are engines of **character growth**: numbers going up, new abilities unlocking, gear improving, choices shaping a build. The genre's pull is the steady, rewarding power curve — the fantasy of becoming stronger. Designing an RPG is designing that progression: satisfying, meaningful, and balanced so growth feels earned but never grindy.

## Stats & Attributes

The numeric foundation — **attributes** (Strength, Agility, Intelligence…) and derived **stats** (health, damage, crit, resistances) that define a character's capabilities. Design considerations:
- **Meaningful stats** — each should visibly matter and support different playstyles; avoid stats nobody invests in.
- **Derived formulas** — how attributes translate to combat numbers (e.g. STR → melee damage). Clear, tunable relationships.
- **Trade-offs** — points/gear spent on one thing not another, forcing build identity.

## XP & Leveling Curves

- **XP → levels → power.** The **leveling curve** (XP needed per level) paces the whole game: too fast trivializes content, too slow grinds. Usually rising costs per level.
- **Level = gate** — content, gear, and abilities gated by level structure the journey.
- **Reward cadence** — leveling should feel steady early (frequent dopamine), stretching later; each level should *give* something (a point, an ability, a stat bump), never feel empty.

## Skill Trees & Builds

**Skill/talent trees** let players spend points to customize their character — the heart of RPG expression:
- **Meaningful choices** — branches that create *distinct* builds (crit-assassin vs tank-bruiser), ideally with tough trade-offs (can't have everything).
- **Synergies** — talents that combine into powerful builds reward theorycrafting.
- **Respec** — allow rebuilding (with friction or freely) so experimentation isn't punished.
- Avoid "illusory choice" (one obviously-correct path) — build diversity is the goal.

## Loot & Gear Progression

Gear is a parallel power axis and a core reward loop:
- **Rarity tiers** and **stat rolls** (see inventory-and-item-systems) create the chase — the dopamine of a better drop.
- **Power creep by design** — gear should steadily improve, with meaningful upgrades and build-defining unique items.
- **The loot loop** — kill → drop → upgrade → tackle harder content → better drops. Pacing drops (not too stingy, not flooding) sustains engagement.

## Damage Formulas & Scaling

- **Damage/defense formulas** — additive vs multiplicative, diminishing returns on defense (so armor never makes you invincible), crit and mitigation. These govern whether the game feels balanced across levels.
- **Scaling** — enemy stats scale with progression; **level-scaling** (enemies match the player) keeps challenge but can undercut the power fantasy if overdone. Balance the feeling of *growing stronger* against *staying challenged*.

## Classes, Roles & Party

- **Classes/archetypes** — predefined builds (warrior/mage/rogue) or freeform; each a distinct fantasy and role.
- **Party roles** — tank/healer/DPS/support (the "holy trinity" and beyond); combat designed so roles are interdependent, making team composition matter.

## Quests & Reward Pacing

Quests structure content and *deliver* rewards (XP, gold, gear, story). Pace them so the player always has a next goal and a steady drip of progression. Mix required and optional; reward exploration and choice.

## The Power Fantasy Curve

The overarching arc: **weak beginnings → mastery**. Early game teaches and challenges with scarce power; mid game blooms into build identity; late game delivers the payoff of a fully-realized, powerful character (and endgame systems to keep growing — paragon levels, gear chase, prestige). Design the whole curve so growth feels *constant, earned, and expressive* — the player is always becoming someone stronger, their way.

Design RPG progression around **meaningful stats, a well-paced leveling curve, expressive skill trees, a satisfying loot loop, and balanced scaling** — all serving a power fantasy where every hour makes the character measurably, personally stronger.
