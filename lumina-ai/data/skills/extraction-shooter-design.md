# Extraction Shooter Design

Extraction shooters (Escape from Tarkov, Hunt: Showdown, the extraction modes proliferating across the industry) build tension around a **high-stakes raid-and-extract loop**: enter a map with your gear, fight PvP and PvE threats for loot, and try to **extract alive** — because if you die, you *lose everything you brought and found*. This persistent risk is the genre's electrifying core.

## The Raid/Extract Loop

The defining structure:
1. **Deploy** into a session/map with the gear (weapons, armor, meds) you *chose to bring* — often bought/found from your persistent stash.
2. **Raid** — fight enemies (other players and AI), scavenge loot, complete objectives, survive.
3. **Extract** — reach an extraction point and get out **alive** to *keep* everything you brought and found. **Die, and you lose it all** (your gear drops for others to loot).

Every raid is a self-contained high-stakes expedition. The loop's genius is that *both* success and failure carry weight.

## Gear-Fear & Persistent Risk

The emotional engine is **persistent loss** ("gear-fear"):
- Because you can **permanently lose everything** on death, every decision is fraught. Bringing good gear means risking it; bringing nothing risks nothing but gains little. This creates intense, sweaty tension found in few genres.
- **Loot stakes** — the gear you find is only *yours if you extract*. Carrying a valuable haul makes the trip out terrifying — do you push for more or play safe with what you have?
- **Investment and attachment** — your gear and stash are hard-won and persistent, so risking them *matters*. This is what makes extraction shooters so gripping (and stressful).

## PvPvE Tension

Extraction shooters mix **players and AI threats**:
- **PvP** — other real players hunting the same loot/objectives are the primary, unpredictable danger. Encounters are lethal and high-stakes (they can take your gear).
- **PvE** — AI enemies add pressure, guard loot, and create noise/chaos that PvP players exploit.
- **Third-partying** — fights attract others; winning a duel only to be picked off by a lurking third party is a signature dynamic. Sound and information discipline are survival skills.

The blend of human unpredictability and environmental threat creates constant, paranoid tension.

## Extraction Points & Timing

The **extraction mechanic** itself is a design tension:
- **Extract points** — specific (sometimes dynamic/random, sometimes contested or costly) locations you must reach to leave. They're natural ambush spots and decision points.
- **Timing** — when to head for extract (with your loot) vs stay for more is the core risk-reward beat. Extract too early (leave value); too late (die with it all).
- Some designs add extraction costs, timers, or conditions to heighten the drama of leaving.

## Risk-Reward: Push or Extract

The heartbeat decision, every raid: **greed vs safety.**
- Push deeper for better loot (more risk of death and loss) or extract now with what you have (bank the gains). This tension permeates every moment.
- Managing it — reading the situation, your gear value, remaining threats — is the core skill and the source of the genre's thrilling, agonizing decisions.

## Meta-Progression & Stash

- **Persistent stash/inventory** — gear, loot, and currency carry between raids; building and protecting your stash is the meta-game.
- **Progression** — traders, crafting, hideout/base upgrades, skills, and quests give long-term goals across raids (see rpg-progression, survival-crafting).
- **Economy** — a player-driven loot economy where scarcity and value drive decisions.

## Balancing Stakes & Frustration

The design razor's edge: **high stakes are the appeal, but losing everything can feel crushing** — especially to newcomers or after bad luck. Mitigations: insurance, secure containers (keep *some* items on death), "scav"/low-risk modes to earn without risking your main gear, and matchmaking. Balancing brutal tension against player retention (not driving people away with pure frustration) is the central challenge.

Design extraction shooters around the **raid-and-extract loop with persistent gear loss**, the **gear-fear tension** that makes every decision weighty, **PvPvE** unpredictability with third-party dynamics, **extraction points** as decision/ambush beats, a **persistent stash and meta-progression**, and — above all — the **push-or-extract risk-reward** that defines every raid — while carefully balancing crushing stakes against frustration. The genre's magic: making loot and survival *actually matter* by letting you truly lose them.
