# Ecology & Ecosystems (THPT Biology)

Ecology studies how **living things interact with each other and their environment**. An **ecosystem** is a community of organisms plus their physical surroundings, functioning together. It's a THPT biology topic (sinh thái học) covering how energy and matter flow through nature and how populations and environments shape each other.

## Levels of Organization

Ecology is studied at nested scales:
- **Organism → population** (same species in an area) → **community** (all species interacting) → **ecosystem** (community + environment) → **biome** (regional ecosystem type) → **biosphere** (all life).
- **Population ecology** — size, density, growth (exponential vs logistic, limited by **carrying capacity** — the max a habitat can sustain), and factors regulating numbers (food, predators, disease, competition).

## Food Chains, Webs & Trophic Levels

Energy and nutrients pass through feeding relationships:
- **Producers** (autotrophs — plants, algae) make energy from sunlight (photosynthesis) — the base of every food chain.
- **Consumers** (heterotrophs) — herbivores (primary), carnivores (secondary/tertiary), omnivores — eat others.
- **Decomposers** (bacteria, fungi) break down dead matter, recycling nutrients.
- **Trophic levels** — feeding positions in the chain. **Food webs** are interconnected chains (more realistic than a single chain).

## Energy Flow & Pyramids

- **Energy flows one way** — from the sun through producers to consumers, lost as heat at each step. It is *not* recycled.
- **~10% rule** — only about 10% of energy passes to the next trophic level (90% lost to metabolism/heat). This is why food chains are short (usually 4–5 links) and why there are fewer top predators.
- **Ecological pyramids** — of energy, biomass, or numbers — show this decrease up the levels. A key exam concept.

## Nutrient Cycles

Unlike energy, **matter cycles** (biogeochemical cycles):
- **Carbon cycle** (photosynthesis/respiration/combustion), **nitrogen cycle** (fixation by bacteria → plants → animals → decomposition), **water cycle**, phosphorus cycle.
- Nutrients are continually recycled between organisms and the environment — decomposers are essential to closing these loops.

## Species Interactions

Relationships between species shape communities:
- **Competition** (−/−) — for shared resources.
- **Predation / herbivory** (+/−) — one benefits, one harmed.
- **Symbiosis** — **mutualism** (+/+, both benefit — bees & flowers), **commensalism** (+/0), **parasitism** (+/−).
These interactions regulate populations and drive adaptation (see evolution).

## Succession & Biodiversity

- **Ecological succession** — communities change over time (primary: bare rock → soil → forest; secondary: after disturbance like fire), progressing toward a stable **climax community**.
- **Biodiversity** — the variety of life; higher biodiversity generally means more stable, resilient ecosystems. Important for ecosystem services and stability.

## Human Impact

- **Pollution, deforestation, overexploitation, habitat loss, and climate change** (see physical-geography-and-climate) disrupt ecosystems, cause extinctions, and break nutrient/energy balances.
- **Conservation and sustainability** — protecting biodiversity and ecosystem function. A major theme, increasingly emphasized in the exam and beyond.

Master THPT ecology via the **levels of organization** (population to biosphere, carrying capacity), **food chains/webs and trophic levels** (producers→consumers→decomposers), **one-way energy flow with the ~10% rule** (pyramids), **cycling of matter** (carbon, nitrogen, water), **species interactions** (competition, predation, symbiosis), **succession and biodiversity**, and **human impact/conservation**. The core idea: ecosystems run on energy flowing through and matter cycling among interacting organisms — and human activity increasingly disrupts that balance.
