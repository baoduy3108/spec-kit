# Economic & Human Geography (Địa lý Kinh tế - Xã hội)

Human geography studies **people and their activities across space** — where and how people live, work, and organize economies. Economic geography focuses on production, trade, and development. Together they explain global and regional patterns of population, cities, and wealth — a core geography subject (Địa lý kinh tế-xã hội).

## Population & Demographics

- **Distribution** — population is unevenly spread (concentrated in fertile plains, coasts, and cities; sparse in deserts, mountains, poles), shaped by physical geography, resources, and history.
- **Demographic structure** — age/sex composition (**population pyramids**); growth from birth/death rates and migration.
- **The demographic transition** — as countries develop, they shift from high birth+death rates → falling death rates (population boom) → falling birth rates (stabilizing/aging). Developed nations face **aging** populations; developing ones have **youthful** ones — with big economic implications.
- **Migration** — rural→urban, and international, driven by push (poverty, conflict) and pull (jobs, safety) factors.

## Urbanization & Settlement

- **Urbanization** — the growing share of people in cities, accelerating with industrialization. **Megacities** and urban sprawl.
- **Settlement patterns** — rural vs urban; the roles and hierarchy of settlements (villages to metropolises).
- **Challenges** — housing, infrastructure, congestion, slums, and planning (see city-builder for the design side).

## Economic Sectors

Economies are divided into sectors:
- **Primary** — extracting raw materials (agriculture, mining, fishing, forestry). Dominant in less-developed economies.
- **Secondary** — manufacturing and processing (industry, construction). The engine of industrialization.
- **Tertiary** — services (retail, finance, education, tourism, health). Dominant in developed economies.
- **(Quaternary)** — knowledge/information (research, IT).
- **Development shift** — economies move from primary → secondary → tertiary as they develop (structural change).

## Globalization & Trade

- **Globalization** — increasing interconnection of economies, cultures, and people (trade, investment, communication, migration).
- **Global supply chains** — production spread across countries (see how Vietnam rose in manufacturing).
- **Trade** — comparative advantage, exports/imports, trade blocs, and the flows of goods, capital, and labor. Benefits and disruptions (winners and losers).

## Development & Inequality

- **Measuring development** — GDP per capita, and broader measures like the **Human Development Index (HDI)** (income + education + health).
- **The development gap** — stark inequality between and within countries (rich/poor, urban/rural, global North/South).
- **Causes and cures** — geography, history (colonialism), institutions, education, and investment shape development; debates over aid, trade, and industrialization strategies.

## Resources & Sustainability

- **Natural resources** — their distribution shapes economies and geopolitics (oil, minerals, water, arable land — renewable vs non-renewable).
- **Sustainability** — balancing development with environmental limits (see physical-geography-and-climate); climate change, resource depletion, and sustainable-development goals are defining challenges.

Understand human and economic geography via **population** (uneven distribution, the demographic transition from youthful to aging, migration), **urbanization** (the rural-to-urban shift and its challenges), **economic sectors** (primary → secondary → tertiary as economies develop), **globalization and trade** (interconnection, supply chains, comparative advantage), **development and inequality** (HDI, the development gap and its causes), and **resources/sustainability**. These spatial patterns of people, cities, and economies — and the inequalities among them — are the core of Địa lý kinh tế-xã hội.
