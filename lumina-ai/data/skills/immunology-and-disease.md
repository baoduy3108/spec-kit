# Immunology & Disease (Advanced Biology)

Immunology studies how the body **defends against disease** — recognizing and destroying pathogens. It's an advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) biology topic, especially relevant post-pandemic, explaining infection, immunity, and how vaccines work.

## Pathogens & Disease

- **Pathogens** — disease-causing agents: **bacteria** (cellular, treatable with antibiotics), **viruses** (non-cellular, hijack host cells — see molecular-genetics), **fungi**, **protists**, **parasites**.
- **Transmission** — airborne, contact, vectors (mosquitoes), contaminated food/water.
- **Infectious vs non-infectious** disease (genetic, lifestyle, deficiency).

## Innate (Non-Specific) Immunity

The first, immediate line of defense — fast but general:
- **Physical/chemical barriers** — skin, mucus, stomach acid, tears (lysozyme) keep pathogens out.
- **Innate cellular response** — if breached, **phagocytes** (white blood cells like macrophages) engulf and destroy pathogens; **inflammation** (redness, heat, swelling) recruits defenses; fever slows pathogens.
- Non-specific — responds the same way to any invader, without memory.

## Adaptive (Specific) Immunity

The targeted, learned response — slower but precise and remembered:
- **Antigens** — molecules on pathogens that the immune system recognizes as foreign.
- **B cells** — produce **antibodies** (proteins) that bind specific antigens, neutralizing pathogens and marking them for destruction (**humoral immunity**).
- **T cells** — **helper T cells** coordinate the response; **cytotoxic (killer) T cells** destroy infected cells (**cell-mediated immunity**).
- **Specificity** — each response is tailored to a particular pathogen.

## Immunological Memory & Vaccination

The key to lasting protection:
- **Memory cells** — after an infection, some B/T cells persist as **memory cells**. On re-exposure, they mount a **faster, stronger secondary response** — often preventing illness entirely. This is **immunity**.
- **Vaccination** — introducing a harmless form/part of a pathogen (or its antigen/mRNA) trains the immune system to make memory cells *without* causing disease. On real exposure, the body responds fast. Vaccines have eradicated/controlled many diseases.
- **Herd immunity** — when enough of a population is immune, disease spread is limited, protecting the vulnerable.

## Immune Disorders & Applications

- **Allergies** — immune overreaction to harmless substances. **Autoimmune diseases** — immune system attacks the body's own cells (type 1 diabetes, lupus).
- **Immunodeficiency** — weakened immunity (HIV/AIDS attacks helper T cells).
- **Antibiotic resistance** — bacteria evolving (see evolution) to survive antibiotics — a major public-health threat from misuse.
- **Applications** — vaccines, monoclonal antibodies, immunotherapy for cancer.

Master immunology via **pathogens** (bacteria, viruses, and how disease spreads), **innate immunity** (fast, general barriers and phagocytes with inflammation), **adaptive immunity** (specific B cells making antibodies and T cells killing infected cells), **immunological memory and vaccination** (memory cells enabling fast secondary responses — the basis of vaccines and herd immunity), and **immune disorders** (allergies, autoimmunity, immunodeficiency, antibiotic resistance). Understanding how the body learns to recognize and remember pathogens is the immunological depth gifted-exam biology — and public health — require.
