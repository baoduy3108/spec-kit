# Physical Geography & Climate (THPT Geography)

Physical geography studies **Earth's natural features and systems** — its landforms, atmosphere, water, and climate — and how they shape (and are shaped by) the planet and life. It's a core THPT geography topic (địa lý tự nhiên), explaining why the world looks and behaves as it does.

## Earth's Structure & Plate Tectonics

- **Earth's layers** — crust (thin, solid), mantle (hot, slowly flowing rock), outer core (liquid), inner core (solid iron). Heat from the core drives geological activity.
- **Plate tectonics** — the crust is broken into **plates** that slowly move on the mantle. Their interactions build the planet's surface:
  - **Convergent** boundaries (collide) → mountains, volcanoes, deep trenches, earthquakes.
  - **Divergent** (separate) → mid-ocean ridges, rift valleys.
  - **Transform** (slide past) → earthquakes (e.g. San Andreas).
- Tectonics explains continents' shapes, mountain ranges, earthquakes, and volcanoes.

## Landforms & Processes

- **Landforms** — mountains, valleys, plains, plateaus, coasts — built by tectonic forces and shaped by **weathering and erosion** (water, wind, ice, gravity breaking down and moving rock) and **deposition**.
- **The rock cycle** — igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic rocks transform over time.
- Rivers, glaciers, wind, and waves continuously sculpt the surface.

## The Atmosphere & Weather

- **The atmosphere** — layers of gases (mostly nitrogen and oxygen) surrounding Earth, holding weather and enabling life.
- **Weather** — short-term atmospheric conditions (temperature, pressure, humidity, wind, precipitation).
- **Drivers** — uneven solar heating creates pressure differences → **wind** and global circulation; the water cycle drives clouds and rain; the **Coriolis effect** (Earth's rotation) curves winds and currents.

## Climate & Climate Zones

- **Climate** — the long-term average weather pattern of a region (weather is today; climate is the decades-long norm).
- **Factors shaping climate** — **latitude** (equator hot, poles cold — the biggest factor), **altitude** (higher = colder), **distance from the sea** (oceans moderate temperature), **ocean currents** (warm/cold currents), and **prevailing winds**.
- **Climate zones** — tropical, arid, temperate, continental, polar — each with characteristic temperature, rainfall, and ecosystems (linking to ecology and agriculture). Vietnam's tropical monsoon climate is an example.

## The Water Cycle & Resources

- **The water cycle** — evaporation → condensation (clouds) → precipitation → collection/runoff → back to the sea; the continuous movement of Earth's water, driving weather and freshwater supply.
- **Natural resources** — water, soil, minerals, forests, energy (renewable: solar, wind, hydro; non-renewable: fossil fuels). Their distribution shapes economies and human settlement (see human geography).

## Climate Change

- **Climate change** — the current long-term warming driven largely by human greenhouse-gas emissions (CO₂ from fossil fuels, deforestation) enhancing the natural greenhouse effect.
- **Impacts** — rising temperatures and seas, extreme weather, shifting rainfall, ecosystem stress (see ecology). A defining global challenge and a growing exam theme, tied to sustainability.

Master THPT physical geography via **Earth's structure and plate tectonics** (building mountains, volcanoes, earthquakes), **landforms and the shaping processes** (weathering, erosion), the **atmosphere and weather** (heating, pressure, wind), **climate and its drivers** (latitude, altitude, oceans, currents → climate zones), the **water cycle and resources**, and **climate change**. The core idea: Earth's interlocking natural systems — solid earth, air, water, and energy from the sun — create the landscapes, climates, and resources that shape our world.
