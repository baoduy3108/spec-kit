# Chemical Bonding & Reactions

Chemistry is the science of how atoms **combine and rearrange**. Bonding explains how atoms join into molecules and materials; reactions explain how those arrangements change. Both flow from one idea: atoms seek stable electron arrangements (full outer shells — see atomic-structure-and-periodic-table).

## Why Atoms Bond

Atoms bond to achieve **stable, full outer electron shells** (the **octet rule** — 8 valence electrons, like noble gases). An atom with a nearly-empty or nearly-full valence shell is reactive; bonding lets it reach stability. Whether it *gives away*, *takes*, or *shares* electrons determines the bond type.

## Types of Bonds

- **Ionic bonds** — a metal *transfers* electrons to a nonmetal, forming charged ions (+ and −) that attract. Produces crystals (like table salt, NaCl), high melting points, conducts when dissolved/molten. Metal + nonmetal.
- **Covalent bonds** — nonmetals *share* electron pairs to fill their shells. Forms discrete molecules (H₂O, CO₂, O₂). Can be single/double/triple bonds. Nonmetal + nonmetal.
- **Metallic bonds** — metal atoms share a "sea" of delocalized electrons, giving metals their conductivity, malleability, and shine.

The bond type (driven by the electronegativity difference — see periodic trends) dictates the substance's properties.

## Molecular Shape & Polarity

- **Shape** — molecules have 3D geometry (VSEPR theory: electron pairs repel to spread out — linear, bent, tetrahedral, etc.). Shape affects properties and reactivity.
- **Polarity** — unequal electron sharing (different electronegativities) makes one end slightly negative, the other positive (a **polar** molecule, like water). Polarity drives solubility ("like dissolves like"), boiling points, and biology (water's polarity is why it's the solvent of life).

## Chemical Reactions & Equations

A **reaction** rearranges atoms, breaking and forming bonds to make new substances:
- **Chemical equations** — reactants → products (2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O).
- **Conservation of mass** — atoms are neither created nor destroyed; they're rearranged. So equations must be **balanced** — the same number of each atom on both sides (the coefficients). Balancing is fundamental chemistry bookkeeping.

## Reaction Types

Common patterns:
- **Synthesis** (combine: A + B → AB), **decomposition** (AB → A + B), **single/double replacement**, **combustion** (fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + energy), **acid-base neutralization**, **redox** (electron transfer — oxidation/reduction). Recognizing the type helps predict products.

## Energy in Reactions

- **Exothermic** — releases energy (usually heat/light); products more stable than reactants (combustion, most spontaneous reactions).
- **Endothermic** — absorbs energy; requires input (photosynthesis, cooking).
- **Activation energy** — the initial "hump" of energy needed to start a reaction; **catalysts** lower it (speeding reactions without being consumed — enzymes in biology). Bond-breaking costs energy; bond-forming releases it; the net determines exo/endothermic.

## Acids & Bases

- **Acids** donate H⁺ (protons), taste sour, low pH; **bases** accept H⁺ (or donate OH⁻), high pH. The **pH scale** (0–14) measures acidity (7 = neutral). Acid + base → salt + water (neutralization). A pervasive, practically important class of reactions.

Reason about chemistry via **why atoms bond** (octet stability), the **bond types** (ionic transfer, covalent sharing, metallic sea) and their properties, **molecular shape and polarity** (driving solubility and behavior), **balanced reaction equations** (conservation of mass), **reaction types and energy** (exo/endothermic, activation energy, catalysts), and **acids/bases** (pH, neutralization). The throughline: chemistry is atoms rearranging electrons and bonds to reach stability.
