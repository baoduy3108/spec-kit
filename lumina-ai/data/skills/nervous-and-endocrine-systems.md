# Nervous & Endocrine Systems (Advanced Biology)

The nervous and endocrine systems are the body's two **control and coordination** systems — one fast and electrical, one slower and chemical. Together they sense the environment, process information, and direct all other systems to maintain homeostasis (see human-physiology-and-organ-systems). An advanced/gifted-exam (chuyên) biology topic.

## Neurons & the Action Potential

- **Neurons** — nerve cells that transmit electrical signals: **dendrites** receive, the **cell body** integrates, the **axon** carries the signal, and terminals pass it on.
- **Resting potential** — a neuron maintains a voltage across its membrane (~−70 mV) via ion pumps (Na⁺/K⁺).
- **Action potential** — a stimulus above threshold triggers a rapid, self-propagating voltage spike: Na⁺ rushes in (depolarization), then K⁺ out (repolarization). It travels down the axon **all-or-nothing** (fixed size; frequency encodes intensity).
- **Myelin** — insulating sheath speeding conduction (saltatory conduction between nodes).

## Synapses & Neurotransmitters

- **Synapse** — the junction between neurons (or neuron and muscle). The signal crosses **chemically**: the action potential triggers release of **neurotransmitters** (dopamine, acetylcholine, serotonin) into the gap; they bind receptors on the next cell, passing (or inhibiting) the signal.
- This chemical relay allows integration, modulation (excitatory/inhibitory), and is the target of many drugs.

## Nervous System Organization

- **Central nervous system (CNS)** — brain and spinal cord; processing and control.
- **Peripheral nervous system (PNS)** — nerves connecting CNS to the body; **somatic** (voluntary, muscles) and **autonomic** (involuntary — sympathetic "fight or flight" vs parasympathetic "rest and digest").
- **The brain** — regions for movement, sensation, emotion, memory, and higher thought.

## Reflexes

- **Reflex arc** — a fast, automatic response bypassing conscious thought: receptor → sensory neuron → spinal cord (relay) → motor neuron → effector (muscle). E.g. pulling your hand from a hot surface before you "feel" it. Protective and rapid.

## Endocrine System & Hormones

The slower, chemical control system:
- **Hormones** — chemical messengers secreted by **glands** into the blood, traveling to target organs with matching receptors. Slower onset but longer-lasting than nerves.
- **Key glands/hormones** — pituitary ("master gland"), thyroid (metabolism), adrenal (adrenaline — stress), pancreas (**insulin/glucagon** — blood sugar), reproductive glands (sex hormones).
- **Regulation** — mostly by **negative feedback** (see feedback in physiology): e.g. insulin lowers blood glucose, glucagon raises it, keeping it stable.

## Nervous vs Endocrine — Coordinated Control

- **Nervous** — fast, electrical, precise, short-lived (immediate reactions).
- **Endocrine** — slow, chemical, widespread, long-lasting (growth, metabolism, reproduction).
- They **integrate** — the hypothalamus links them (controlling the pituitary), and both maintain homeostasis. Example: stress triggers both a fast nervous response and adrenaline release.
