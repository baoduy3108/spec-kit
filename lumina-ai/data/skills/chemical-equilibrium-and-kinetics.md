# Chemical Equilibrium & Kinetics (Advanced Chemistry)

Equilibrium asks *how far* a reaction goes; kinetics asks *how fast*. Together they're central to advanced (chuyên) and olympiad chemistry — quantitative, conceptual, and full of problem-solving. Many reactions are **reversible**, settling into a dynamic balance you can predict and shift.

## Reversible Reactions & Equilibrium

- Many reactions proceed *both ways*: aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD. **Equilibrium** is when forward and reverse rates become equal — concentrations stop changing (but reactions continue: **dynamic** equilibrium).
- It's a *balance*, not a stop — reactants and products coexist in steady proportions.

## The Equilibrium Constant

**K = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / [A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ** (products over reactants, each to its coefficient power) at equilibrium:
- **Large K** → products favored (reaction goes far); **small K** → reactants favored. K depends only on **temperature**.
- **Kc** (concentrations) vs **Kp** (partial pressures for gases). The **reaction quotient Q** (same expression, any moment) vs K tells you which way the reaction will shift (Q<K → forward; Q>K → reverse).
- ICE tables (Initial-Change-Equilibrium) solve for equilibrium concentrations — a core quantitative skill.

## Le Chatelier's Principle

**If you disturb a system at equilibrium, it shifts to counteract the change:**
- **Add reactant / remove product** → shifts forward. **Add product** → shifts back.
- **Increase pressure** (gases) → shifts toward fewer gas moles.
- **Increase temperature** → shifts in the endothermic direction (absorbs heat); for exothermic reactions, heat is a "product."
- **Catalysts** speed both directions equally — reach equilibrium faster but **don't shift** it.
Predicting these shifts (le Chatelier) is a staple exam skill (and industrially vital — Haber process).

## Reaction Rates & Rate Laws

Kinetics quantifies **speed**:
- **Rate** = change in concentration per time.
- **Rate law**: rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ — the **orders** (m, n) are found *experimentally* (not from coefficients!), giving the reaction **order** (zero, first, second). k is the rate constant.
- **Integrated rate laws** give concentration vs time; first-order reactions have a constant **half-life** (like radioactive decay — see exponentials-and-logarithms).

## Activation Energy & Arrhenius

- **Activation energy (Ea)** — the energy barrier reactants must overcome; higher Ea = slower reaction.
- **Arrhenius equation**: k = A·e^(−Ea/RT) — rate constant rises sharply with **temperature** (more molecules exceed Ea — connects to the Maxwell-Boltzmann tail) and falls with higher Ea.
- **Energy profile diagrams** — reactants → transition state (peak) → products; the barrier height is Ea, and the reactant-product energy difference is ΔH.

## Catalysts & Collision Model

- **Collision theory** — reactions need molecules to collide with enough energy (≥ Ea) *and* correct orientation. Rate depends on collision frequency and the fraction with sufficient energy.
- **Factors speeding reactions** — higher concentration/pressure (more collisions), higher temperature (more energetic collisions), larger surface area, and **catalysts**.
- **Catalysts** provide an alternative pathway with **lower Ea**, speeding the reaction *without being consumed* (enzymes in biology). They don't change K or ΔH — only the rate.

Master advanced chemistry equilibrium and kinetics via **reversible reactions and dynamic equilibrium**, the **equilibrium constant K** (with Q, ICE tables for calculations), **Le Chatelier's principle** (predicting shifts under concentration/pressure/temperature changes), **rate laws** (experimentally-determined orders), **activation energy and Arrhenius** (temperature dependence), and the **collision model with catalysts** (lowering Ea). These quantitative, predictive tools — the *how far* and *how fast* of reactions — are exactly what gifted-exam/Olympiad chemistry tests deeply.
