# Environmental Science & Sustainability

Environmental science studies how humans interact with the natural world and the consequences — pollution, climate change, and resource depletion — and how to live **sustainably** within Earth's limits. It's an interdisciplinary, increasingly vital field bridging biology, chemistry, geography, and economics.

## Ecosystems & Biodiversity

- **Ecosystems** provide life-supporting **services** — clean air/water, food, pollination, climate regulation, nutrient cycling (see ecology-and-ecosystems).
- **Biodiversity** — the variety of life; higher biodiversity means more resilient, stable ecosystems.
- **Threats** — habitat destruction, overexploitation, invasive species, and pollution drive **extinctions** at an alarming rate (a "sixth mass extinction"). Conservation aims to protect species and habitats.

## Pollution

Contamination harming the environment and health:
- **Air pollution** — from burning fossil fuels (smog, particulates, acid rain, greenhouse gases).
- **Water pollution** — sewage, industrial waste, agricultural runoff (fertilizers → eutrophication), plastics.
- **Soil and plastic pollution, and toxic waste** — persistent contaminants accumulating in food chains (bioaccumulation).
- Impacts human health, wildlife, and ecosystems.

## Climate Change (The Defining Challenge)

- **Cause** — human greenhouse-gas emissions (CO₂ from fossil fuels, methane, deforestation) enhance the natural **greenhouse effect**, trapping heat (see physical-geography-and-climate).
- **Effects** — rising global temperatures, melting ice and rising seas, more extreme weather, shifting rainfall and ecosystems, ocean acidification.
- **Response** — **mitigation** (cutting emissions — renewable energy, efficiency) and **adaptation** (adjusting to impacts). International agreements (Paris Agreement) and net-zero goals.

## Resource Depletion

- **Non-renewable resources** (fossil fuels, minerals) are finite and depleting; **renewable resources** (forests, fisheries, water) can be exhausted if overused faster than they regenerate.
- **The tragedy of the commons** — shared resources tend to be overexploited without cooperation.
- Growing population and consumption strain resources — driving the need for efficiency and circular economies.

## Renewable Energy

- **Renewables** — solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, biomass — sustainable, low-carbon alternatives to fossil fuels.
- **Transition challenges** — intermittency (storage), cost, infrastructure — but rapidly growing and central to decarbonization.

## Sustainable Development

- **Sustainability** — meeting present needs *without compromising future generations'* ability to meet theirs (the Brundtland definition).
- **Three pillars** — environmental, economic, and social — balanced together.
- **Sustainable Development Goals**, circular economy (reduce/reuse/recycle), conservation, and green technology are the path forward.
- **Individual and systemic action** — both personal choices and policy/industry change matter.

Understand environmental science via **ecosystems and biodiversity** (services and threats/conservation), **pollution** (air, water, soil, plastics, and their health/ecological impacts), **climate change** (greenhouse-gas causes, wide-ranging effects, mitigation and adaptation), **resource depletion** (finite resources, the tragedy of the commons), **renewable energy** (the low-carbon transition), and **sustainable development** (meeting needs within limits, balancing environment/economy/society). The core message — human activity is straining Earth's systems, and sustainability is the response — makes this among the most important knowledge for the future.
